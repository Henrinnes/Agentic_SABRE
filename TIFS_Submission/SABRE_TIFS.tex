\begin{abstract}
Ransomware has become one of the most sophisticated and economically
devastating categories of malware, characterised by rapid mutation,
behavioural polymorphism, and deliberate evasion of
machine-learning-based detectors.
In this paper we present \textbf{Extended Agentic SABRE} (\textbf{SABRE+}):
a three-agent, uncertainty-aware, neuro-symbolic framework for adaptive
ransomware detection that augments the previously proposed two-agent
system with a dedicated \emph{Knowledge Agent} trained on
practitioner-grounded threat-intelligence discourse.
SABRE+ introduces three principled advances beyond the two-agent baseline.
First, a Knowledge Agent operates on \emph{RansKnow}, a novel
307-sample dataset derived from YouTube security-practitioner transcripts
and structured according to MITRE ATT\&CK tactics, tool signatures, and
platform indicators; the agent achieves 0.9550 accuracy and 0.9950
AUC on balanced evaluation.
Second, a \emph{Peer Consultation Protocol} enables any agent whose
epistemic uncertainty exceeds a per-agent threshold $\kappa_{\mathrm{local}}$
to adjust its probability estimate via inverse-uncertainty-weighted peer
scores, without modifying its uncertainty---a design that preserves the
integrity of downstream triage.
Third, an \emph{Expert Feedback Loop} closes the human-in-the-loop
cycle by routing analyst corrections back to incrementally retrain the
Decision MLP, preventing performance decay under concept drift without
catastrophic forgetting.
SABRE+ fuses all three agents into a six-dimensional score vector,
applies Bayesian threshold optimisation (Tree-structured Parzen Estimator)
to a composite objective balancing balanced accuracy and escalation rate,
and enforces a four-region triage policy (AUTO-CONTAIN / ESCALATE /
ALLOW) governed by a fused risk score and $\smax = \max(\sigma_\sem,
\sigma_\beh, \sigma_\know)$.
Empirical evaluation on three independent datasets confirms that each
agent discriminates reliably within its modality, that peer consultation
reduces uncertainty-induced misclassification under high-$\sigma$
conditions, and that the knowledge agent provides a complementary signal
absent from both static and runtime representations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{R}{ansomware} continues to evolve at a pace that outstrips
the deployment cycle of conventional detection systems.
Modern families such as LockBit~3.0, ALPHV/BlackCat, and RansomHub
employ code polymorphism, living-off-the-land techniques, and multi-stage
delivery chains that are specifically engineered to circumvent both
signature-based and machine-learning-based
defences~\cite{razaulla2023age,fernando2020study}.
Enterprise environments therefore face two compounding problems:
(i)~statistical detectors trained on known malware degrade rapidly under
concept drift as attacker tooling evolves~\cite{Ceschin2022ConceptDrift};
(ii)~static classifiers that output a single point prediction cannot
communicate their confidence to downstream response systems, forcing
analysts to choose between aggressive automatic containment
(high false-positive cost) and conservative manual review
(high analyst workload and delayed response)~\cite{Doan2024uncertainBay}.

Prior work on uncertainty-aware ransomware detection has shown that Monte
Carlo Dropout (MC Dropout)~\cite{gal2016dropout} can quantify epistemic
uncertainty for individual binary classifiers, and that this signal can
be used to trigger human escalation when the model is
unreliable~\cite{kabuye2025explainable}.
A two-agent SABRE architecture fusing semantic
(PE-embedding-based) and behavioural (runtime-telemetry-based) signals
with an uncertainty-aware triage policy has demonstrated that
automatic containment and human escalation can be separated by a
risk--uncertainty decision boundary, reducing false escalation rates
while preserving high recall~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa}.
However, two fundamental gaps remain.

\textbf{Gap~1 -- Knowledge blindness.}
Both the semantic and the behavioural agents reason entirely from
statistical regularities in measurement data.
Neither incorporates the operational knowledge accumulated by
security practitioners: which ATT\&CK tactics are most commonly paired
with ransomware deployment, which tools (Cobalt Strike, Rclone, BloodHound,
etc.) appear in attack chains, or which platforms are primary targets.
This knowledge is publicly available in threat-intelligence briefings,
security conference presentations, and analyst-authored YouTube transcripts,
yet it has not previously been operationalised as an agent-level signal in
a multi-agent detection framework.

\textbf{Gap~2 -- Agent isolation.}
In the two-agent SABRE, agents produce independent predictions that are
fused passively through a learned MLP.
An agent operating under high epistemic uncertainty contributes noise to
the fusion layer just as confidently as an agent with low uncertainty,
because there is no mechanism for agents to communicate their reliability to
one another before fusion.
Under distributional shift---new ransomware families that appear semantically
unusual but follow canonical ATT\&CK patterns---the most reliable agent
cannot assist the uncertain one, and the fused prediction degrades.

This paper addresses both gaps with three contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Knowledge Agent and RansKnow Dataset.}
We introduce a third agent---the Knowledge CNN---operating on a new
23-feature threat-intelligence dataset (RansKnow) derived from
307~YouTube security-practitioner transcripts.
Features encode MITRE ATT\&CK tactic frequencies, ransomware-ecosystem
tool mentions, and platform indicators.
The agent achieves 0.9550 accuracy and 0.9950~AUC on balanced held-out
evaluation, providing a modality-orthogonal signal that is independent
of both PE structure and runtime telemetry.

\item \textbf{Peer Consultation Protocol.}
We formalise and implement an inter-agent consultation mechanism:
when an agent's MC Dropout uncertainty $\sigma_i$ exceeds a
per-agent threshold $\kappa_{\mathrm{local}}$, its probability estimate
is adjusted via an inverse-uncertainty-weighted mean over all agents,
including itself.
Crucially, uncertainty is never modified---only the probability propagated
to the fusion layer is updated---preserving the downstream triage
policy's ability to measure true epistemic spread.

\item \textbf{Expert Feedback Loop.}
We design and formalise a continual-adaptation mechanism in which analyst
corrections to AUTO-CONTAIN and ESCALATE decisions are fed back to
incrementally fine-tune the Decision MLP through a
combined new-sample and experience-replay loss, with reservoir-sampled
memory to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Ransomware Detection}
Early detection systems relied on static signatures and
API call sequences~\cite{fernando2020study}.
The transition to deep learning brought CNN and LSTM architectures
operating on system-call traces, filesystem activity, and network
flows~\cite{zahoora2022Deep,alraizza2023ransomware}, achieving strong
in-distribution performance but failing under distributional shift.
Survey work~\cite{razaulla2023age} identifies concept drift as the
primary operational challenge for deployed detectors.
FeSAD~\cite{Fernando2024FeSAD} addresses drift with a feature-selection
framework but lacks uncertainty-aware decision logic.
LLM-LADE~\cite{ZhangLZYHY25} leverages large language models for
log-based anomaly detection with partial symbolic reasoning, but
does not quantify uncertainty or produce triage decisions.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification in Security}
Bayesian deep learning has been applied to network intrusion
detection~\cite{Doan2024uncertainBay}, malware
classification~\cite{kabuye2025explainable}, and vulnerability
assessment.
MC Dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout} is the most practical approach for
production systems, approximating Bayesian inference with a single
trained model at the cost of $T$ forward passes per sample.
Temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration} corrects the
overconfidence of trained neural classifiers and is used here as a
post-hoc calibration step.
The critical missing link in prior work is the
\emph{operational use} of uncertainty: existing systems report it but
do not use it to govern agent behaviour or inter-agent communication.

\subsection{Knowledge-Augmented Detection}
Neuro-symbolic methods integrate structured knowledge with
statistical models to improve generalisation under distributional
shift~\cite{Ispahany2024Survey}.
In the ransomware domain, ATT\&CK-aligned feature engineering has
been used for rule-based pre-filtering~\cite{razaulla2023age}, but has
not been coupled with learned uncertainty estimation.
The RansKnow dataset introduced here is the first knowledge resource
for ransomware detection derived from structured analysis of practitioner
video transcripts.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Security Frameworks}
Multi-agent approaches to intrusion detection decompose detection
into specialised sub-agents whose outputs are
combined~\cite{MultiAgentAdaptiveDetection2023}.
Soltani et al.~\cite{MultiAgentAdaptiveDetection2023} propose an
adaptive multi-agent intrusion detection system but do not equip
agents with uncertainty estimates or inter-agent communication.
The two-agent SABRE baseline~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa} demonstrated
the benefit of modality-specific agents with uncertainty-aware fusion,
but agents remain isolated.
SABRE+ adds a third modality and a formal peer consultation protocol.

\subsection{Continual Learning in Threat Detection}
Concept drift under evolving adversarial strategies requires detectors
to adapt without retraining from scratch~\cite{Ceschin2022ConceptDrift}.
Experience replay~\cite{rolnick2019experience} and elastic weight
consolidation prevent catastrophic forgetting during incremental updates.
SABRE+'s Expert Feedback Loop combines both techniques in a lightweight
online adaptation layer targeting only the Decision MLP---the shallowest
and fastest-to-retrain component---while leaving the agent backbones frozen.

\section{Threat Model and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{Threat Model}
We consider a defender operating an enterprise endpoint detection and
response (EDR) pipeline against an adversary deploying ransomware with
the following capabilities:
(i) binary polymorphism and metamorphism to evade signature matching;
(ii) living-off-the-land (LotL) techniques to blend runtime telemetry
with benign system activity;
(iii) gradual deployment (slow encryption, staged loaders) to defeat
burst-based behavioural detectors;
(iv) access to the defender's detection policy \emph{only} through
repeated probing, not to model weights or training data.
We do \emph{not} assume the adversary can suppress ATT\&CK-aligned tool
usage (Cobalt Strike for lateral movement, Rclone for exfiltration,
etc.) because these tools serve operational purposes the adversary
cannot easily replace.
We do not claim to prevent zero-day exploits or fully novel
attack chains absent from the training distribution.

\subsection{Monte Carlo Dropout Inference}
Given a neural network $f_\theta$ with Dropout layers, MC Dropout
approximates the posterior predictive distribution by performing $T$
stochastic forward passes at inference time.
For a feature vector $\mathbf{x}$:
\begin{align}
\bar{p} &= \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} f_{\theta}^{(t)}(\mathbf{x}),
\label{eq:mc_mean}\\
\sigma  &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(f_{\theta}^{(t)}(\mathbf{x})
           - \bar{p}\right)^2}.
\label{eq:mc_std}
\end{align}
Here $\bar{p}\in[0,1]$ is the predictive mean probability and
$\sigma\in[0,\infty)$ is the empirical standard deviation across passes,
serving as a proxy for epistemic uncertainty.

\subsection{Score Vector}
Each agent $i\in\{\sem,\beh,\know\}$ produces a tuple
$(\bar{p}_i, \sigma_i)$.
After peer consultation the six-dimensional score vector is:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\bar{p}_\sem^* \\ \bar{p}_\beh^* \\ \bar{p}_\know^* \\
\sigma_\sem   \\ \sigma_\beh   \\ \sigma_\know
\end{bmatrix}
\in \mathbb{R}^6,
\label{eq:scorevec}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{p}_i^*$ denotes the post-consultation probability
(equal to $\bar{p}_i$ when consultation did not fire for agent $i$)
and all $\sigma_i$ are the original MC Dropout standard deviations.

\section{Extended Agentic SABRE Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{System Overview}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/Prop_Agentic_Sabre_27_04_2026_3Agents_Fullpipeline.png}
  \caption{Extended Agentic SABRE pipeline. Three modality-specific base
  detectors (Semantic CNN, Behavioural CNN, Knowledge CNN) produce
  mean--uncertainty pairs via MC Dropout.
  The Peer Consultation module adjusts agent probabilities under high
  local uncertainty before scores enter the Score Pool.
  CTGAN augments the pool during training.
  A Decision MLP, tuned by Bayesian optimisation and calibrated by
  temperature scaling, produces the fused risk estimate $\phat$.
  The Triage Policy maps $(\phat, \smax)$ to one of four decisions.
  Analyst corrections from the Expert Feedback Loop incrementally
  retrain the Decision MLP.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} illustrates the complete pipeline.
The system decomposes detection into three specialised agents, each
operating on a distinct modality: static PE-header semantics, runtime
memory and I/O telemetry, and practitioner threat-intelligence discourse.
Agents are trained independently and frozen after initial training.
At inference, each agent performs $T$ MC Dropout forward passes,
producing a predictive mean $\bar{p}_i$ and uncertainty $\sigma_i$.
The Peer Consultation module conditionally adjusts agent probabilities
using inverse-uncertainty-weighted peer scores.
All six values are concatenated into $\mathbf{v}$, optionally augmented
with CTGAN-synthesised score vectors during training, and passed to a
Decision MLP whose thresholds are optimised by Bayesian search.
The Triage Policy maps the output to AUTO-CONTAIN, ESCALATE, or ALLOW.

\subsection{Semantic Agent}

The Semantic Agent operates on 384-dimensional PE-header embeddings
derived from the RDset dataset~\cite{kabuye2025explainable}.
Embeddings are computed from structured metadata (import tables, section
entropy, PE flags, version strings) and capture the static representational
fingerprint of each executable.
The agent architecture is a five-layer CNN over the embedding vector
with ReLU activations, batch normalisation, and a Dropout layer
($p_{\mathrm{drop}}=0.3$) before the final sigmoid unit.
At inference, $T=10$ MC Dropout passes yield $(\bar{p}_\sem, \sigma_\sem)$.
From the trained scaler statistics, $\bar{\sigma}_\sem \approx 2.75
\times 10^{-8}$, confirming near-deterministic confidence on the
RDset distribution---a consequence of the well-separated class-conditional
embedding clusters documented in~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa}.

\subsection{Behavioural Agent}

The Behavioural Agent operates on 16-dimensional runtime telemetry
features extracted from the RanSMAP dataset~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa},
capturing LBA/GPA variance, memory operation counts, and I/O entropy
statistics.
The architecture mirrors the Semantic CNN with a 1D-CNN backbone and
MC Dropout.
The trained scaler indicates $\bar{\sigma}_\beh \approx 0.0138$
($\mathrm{std}\approx 0.0258$), reflecting the greater behavioural
overlap between benign and ransomware-infected hosts compared to the
static embedding space.
On balanced held-out evaluation (1000 samples per class) the agent
achieves accuracy 0.9945 and AUC 0.9967; the imbalanced evaluation
on the full RanSMAP class distribution (598:1 malicious:benign ratio)
yields AUC~0.6030~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa}, confirming that rank-based
separation is substantially harder under extreme class imbalance.

\subsection{Knowledge Agent and RansKnow Dataset}
\label{subsec:knowledge_agent}

\subsubsection{RansKnow Dataset}
RansKnow is a novel dataset of 307~samples (140 ransomware-relevant,
167 benign) derived from structured analysis of YouTube security
practitioner content, including threat-intelligence briefings, DFIR
(digital forensics and incident response) walkthroughs, and
ransomware family deep-dives published by analyst channels.
Each sample corresponds to one video and is represented by
23~structured features (Table~\ref{tab:ransknow_features}):
10~MITRE ATT\&CK tactic frequency counts, 8~ransomware-ecosystem
tool mention counts, 3~platform signal indicators, and 2~aggregate
mention totals.
Labels are derived heuristically:
a sample is \emph{ransomware-relevant} if
$\texttt{Tactic\_Total\_Mentions} > 10$ or
$\texttt{Tool\_Total\_Mentions} > 2$,
reflecting the empirical observation that legitimate ransomware
analyses consistently reference both high-tactic-frequency deployment
chains and specific attacker tooling.

\subsubsection{Architecture}
The Knowledge CNN operates on the 23-dimensional feature vector.
The architecture consists of a 1D convolution layer
(16 filters, kernel size 3, same padding), ReLU activation,
max-pooling (stride 2), Dropout ($p_{\mathrm{drop}}=0.3$), a
flatten layer, and two fully connected layers
(64 hidden units, sigmoid output).
MC Dropout is applied adaptively: passes continue from a minimum of
$T_{\min}=5$ until the running standard deviation of
$\sigma_{\know}$ converges within tolerance $\delta=10^{-3}$,
capped at $T_{\max}=50$.
Trained scaler statistics indicate
$\bar{\sigma}_{\know} \approx 0.0499$ ($\mathrm{std}\approx 0.0734$),
reflecting higher epistemic uncertainty than the Semantic Agent but
comparable to the Behavioural Agent---consistent with the modest
dataset size.

\subsubsection{Complementarity}
The three agents cover distinct attack surfaces.
The Semantic Agent is strong on known-family PE variants but blind to
novel polymorphic samples with unfamiliar byte patterns.
The Behavioural Agent captures runtime encryption dynamics but may be
confused by LotL techniques that generate benign-looking telemetry.
The Knowledge Agent is strong precisely when practitioners have
documented a deployment chain---i.e., for well-known campaigns with
ATT\&CK-mapped tooling---irrespective of PE structure or runtime
behaviour.
Together, the three agents provide overlapping but non-identical
coverage of the ransomware detection surface.

\subsection{Peer Consultation Protocol}
\label{subsec:peer_consultation}

\subsubsection{Motivation}
An agent under high epistemic uncertainty ($\sigma_i \gg 0$) should
not contribute to fusion with the same weight as a confident peer.
The standard MLP fusion layer cannot distinguish this case because it
receives probabilities $\bar{p}_i$ without access to per-agent
uncertainty at the feature level.
Peer consultation resolves this by \emph{adjusting} $\bar{p}_i$
before fusion whenever $\sigma_i > \kappa_{\mathrm{local}}$,
using peer scores weighted by peer confidence.

\subsubsection{Formal Definition}
Let $\mathcal{A} = \{\sem, \beh, \know\}$ denote the agent set
and $\mathcal{P}_i = \mathcal{A} \setminus \{i\}$ the peers of
agent $i$.
For a sample $\mathbf{x}$, define
\begin{equation}
w_j = \frac{1}{\sigma_j + \epsilon}, \quad j \in \mathcal{A},
\label{eq:peer_weights}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ prevents division by zero.
Consultation fires for agent $i$ if $\sigma_i > \kappa_{\mathrm{local},i}$.
The adjusted probability is:
\begin{equation}
\bar{p}_i^* = \frac{w_i \bar{p}_i + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_i}
              w_j \bar{p}_j}{w_i + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_i} w_j}.
\label{eq:consultation}
\end{equation}
If $\sigma_i \leq \kappa_{\mathrm{local},i}$, then $\bar{p}_i^* = \bar{p}_i$.

\textbf{Uncertainty invariance.}
By construction, $\sigma_i$ is never modified by consultation.
The original MC Dropout standard deviation is always propagated to
$\mathbf{v}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:scorevec}), preserving the
semantics of $\smax$ in the triage policy.

\subsubsection{Properties}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Confidence-preserving}: a highly confident peer
  ($\sigma_j \approx 0$) dominates the weighted average, pulling
  the uncertain agent toward the peer's estimate.
\item \emph{Self-inclusive}: agent $i$ retains weight $w_i$ in the
  weighted mean, so consultation cannot invert a strongly confident
  uncertain agent.
\item \emph{Threshold-gated}: consultation is a no-op when all
  agents are confident, incurring no computational overhead beyond
  the threshold comparison.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Score Pool and CTGAN Augmentation}
\label{subsec:ctgan}

After consultation, the six-dimensional score vector
$\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^6$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:scorevec}) is collected
in a Score Pool.
During training, CTGAN~\cite{xu2019ctgan} is applied to the pool to
synthesise additional score vectors, preserving correlations between
agent probabilities and uncertainties.
CTGAN augmentation addresses the scarcity of high-uncertainty samples
in balanced training data and prevents the Decision MLP from being
trained entirely on well-separated score configurations.
At inference, no CTGAN augmentation is applied.

\subsection{Feature Fusion and Decision MLP}
\label{subsec:decision_mlp}

The Decision MLP $f: \mathbb{R}^6 \rightarrow [0,1]$ takes the
(possibly consultation-adjusted) score vector $\mathbf{v}$ as input
and produces the fused risk estimate $\phat$:
\begin{equation}
\phat = f(\bar{p}_\sem^*, \bar{p}_\beh^*, \bar{p}_\know^*,
          \sigma_\sem, \sigma_\beh, \sigma_\know).
\label{eq:fused}
\end{equation}
The network uses two hidden layers (64 and 32 units), ReLU activations,
and a sigmoid output.
Inputs are standardised using a fitted StandardScaler; scaler statistics
from the trained checkpoint are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:scaler_stats}.

The $\bar{p}_\know$ distribution (mean $\approx 0.45$, std $\approx 0.44$)
reflects the high variability of discourse-based signals: some
security videos discuss ransomware in depth (high $\bar{p}_\know$),
others cover unrelated topics (low $\bar{p}_\know$).
The nearly zero $\sigma_\sem$ confirms that the semantic embedding
space provides a well-separated distribution under the current RDset
training regime.

\subsection{Bayesian Threshold Optimisation}
\label{subsec:bayesian_opt}

The triage policy is governed by four scalar thresholds:
$\tau, \kappa, \tauh, \klow$ (defined in
Section~\ref{subsec:triage_policy}).
Thresholds are jointly optimised on a held-out validation set using
a Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE):
\begin{equation}
(\tau^*, \kappa^*, \tauh^*, \klow^*) =
\arg\max_{\tau,\kappa,\tauh,\klow}
\bigl[\balAcc(\tau,\kappa) - \lambda\,\escRate(\tau,\kappa)\bigr],
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
subject to $\tau \leq \tauh$ and $\klow \leq \kappa$.
The trade-off parameter $\lambda = 0.5$ balances detection
accuracy against escalation load.
The optimised low-escalation policy yields:
$\tau^* = 0.2330$, $\kappa^* = 0.1969$,
$\tauh^* = 0.8545$, $\klow^* = 0.1969$.

\subsection{Temperature Calibration}
\label{subsec:temperature}

Post-hoc temperature scaling is applied to $\phat$:
\begin{equation}
\phat_{\mathrm{cal}} = \sigma\!\left(\frac{\ln
\frac{\phat}{1-\phat}}{T_{\mathrm{cal}}}\right),
\label{eq:temperature}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{cal}} > 0$ is fitted on the validation set by
minimising negative log-likelihood.
Temperature scaling preserves the threshold-relative ordering of
$\phat$ while reducing overconfidence, improving the semantic
alignment between the fused risk score and the binary labels used
by the triage policy.

\subsection{Triage Policy}
\label{subsec:triage_policy}

The global uncertainty aggregate is:
\begin{equation}
\smax = \max(\sigma_\sem, \sigma_\beh, \sigma_\know).
\label{eq:sigmamax}
\end{equation}
The triage policy maps $(\phat_{\mathrm{cal}}, \smax)$ to a decision:
\begin{align}
\text{AUTO-CONTAIN}  &\quad \text{if}\quad
  \phat_{\mathrm{cal}} \geq \tauh \;\wedge\; \smax \leq \klow,
\label{eq:auto}\\
\text{ESCALATE}      &\quad \text{if}\quad
  \phat_{\mathrm{cal}} \geq \tau \;\vee\; \smax > \kappa,
\label{eq:esc}\\
\text{ALLOW}         &\quad \text{otherwise.}
\label{eq:allow}
\end{align}
The four-region geometry ensures that:
(a)~automatic containment requires both high risk \emph{and}
low uncertainty;
(b)~human escalation is triggered by either elevated risk
\emph{or} elevated uncertainty;
(c)~samples with low risk and low uncertainty are autonomously
allowed without analyst involvement.
Threshold ordering $\tau \leq \tauh$ and $\klow \leq \kappa$
guarantees consistency (AUTO-CONTAIN is always a strict subset of
the ESCALATE region before the $\smax \leq \klow$ condition
is applied).

\section{Expert Feedback Loop}
\label{sec:feedback}

\subsection{Architecture}

The Expert Feedback Loop closes the human-in-the-loop cycle.
Whenever a sample receives an ESCALATE or AUTO-CONTAIN decision,
the analyst reviews it and provides a correction label
$\tilde{y} \in \{0, 1\}$ (0 = benign, 1 = ransomware).
Correction labels are accumulated in a correction queue
$\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{new}}$.

\subsection{Incremental Retraining}

The Decision MLP is fine-tuned on a mixed loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}} =
\underbrace{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{new}}|}
\sum_{(\mathbf{v},\tilde{y}) \in \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{new}}}
\mathrm{BCE}(f(\mathbf{v}), \tilde{y})}_{\text{new-sample loss}}
+\;\gamma \cdot
\underbrace{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}
\sum_{(\mathbf{v},y) \in \mathcal{B}}
\mathrm{BCE}(f(\mathbf{v}), y)}_{\text{replay loss}},
\label{eq:feedback_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}$ is a replay buffer of size $N_{\mathrm{replay}}$
updated by reservoir sampling, and $\gamma \geq 0$ controls the
forgetting--adaptation trade-off.
Agent backbone weights are frozen: only the Decision MLP is updated.
This design limits parameter exposure, reduces catastrophic forgetting
risk to the shallowest component, and keeps update latency low
(the Decision MLP has $\leq 4{,}000$ parameters).

\subsection{Reservoir Sampling}
Each round, the replay buffer $\mathcal{B}$ is updated by reservoir
sampling~\cite{vitter1985random}: for each new sample at position
$n$, it replaces a uniformly random element of $\mathcal{B}$ with
probability $N_{\mathrm{replay}} / n$.
This provides an unbiased uniform sample over all historical
correction labels, preventing recent-class bias.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:feedback_loop} summarises the full feedback process.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Expert Feedback Loop (one round)}
\label{alg:feedback_loop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Correction queue $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{new}}$,
         replay buffer $\mathcal{B}$, MLP $f_\theta$,
         learning rate $\eta$, replay weight $\gamma$
\For{each epoch}
  \State Compute $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}$
         (Eq.~\eqref{eq:feedback_loss})
  \State $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta
         \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}$
\EndFor
\For{$(\mathbf{v}, \tilde{y}) \in \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{new}}$}
  \State Update $\mathcal{B}$ via reservoir sampling
\EndFor
\State Clear $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{new}}$
\State \Return Updated $f_\theta$, updated $\mathcal{B}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}

\textbf{RDset.}
138,047 PE-file records (ransom = 96,724; benign = 41,323).
Each record carries a 384-dimensional semantic embedding derived
from structured PE-header metadata.
Evaluation uses stratified sampling (2000 per class).

\textbf{RanSMAP.}
751,588 runtime monitoring records (ransom = 750,329; benign = 1,259;
native 598:1 class ratio).
Each record contains 16~I/O and memory telemetry features.
Balanced evaluation uses 1000 per class;
imbalanced evaluation uses the full natural distribution.

\textbf{RansKnow.}
307~YouTube-sourced practitioner discourse samples
(ransom-relevant = 140; benign = 167).
Each sample carries 23 ATT\&CK/tool/platform features.
Evaluation uses all available samples (100 per class where balanced).

All three datasets are entirely independent; no executable or video
appears in more than one.
Because no single dataset contains all three modalities simultaneously,
fusion-level evaluation in Section~\ref{sec:results} uses the most
reliable modality as the label source with cross-modality inputs
noted explicitly.

\subsection{Baselines}
We compare against:
(i)~the original two-agent SABRE~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa};
(ii)~the single-agent uncertainty-aware detector from~\cite{kabuye2025explainable};
(iii)~LLM-LADE~\cite{ZhangLZYHY25};
(iv)~FeSAD~\cite{Fernando2024FeSAD};
(v)~the multi-agent adaptive detection system of~\cite{MultiAgentAdaptiveDetection2023}.
Baselines are evaluated under identical data splits where possible.
Results for external baselines not available for direct re-evaluation
are taken from the reported literature values.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We report accuracy, balanced accuracy, ROC-AUC, precision, recall,
and F1-score.
For triage systems we additionally report the escalation rate
(fraction of samples routed to ESCALATE) and AUTO-CONTAIN rate.
Expected Calibration Error (ECE) is computed for uncertainty quality.
Uncertainty quality is assessed via mean $\sigma$ per class
(lower for well-separated classes, higher for overlapping ones is
the expected direction).

\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:security}

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness of the Semantic Agent}
Counterfactual analysis on RDset~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa}
shows that flipping the Semantic Agent's prediction requires a mean
$\ell_2$ perturbation of $\|\Delta\mathbf{z}\|_2 = 3.27$ in
embedding space (100\% flip success rate), with low variance.
Under the threat model, an adversary must craft a PE binary whose
384-dimensional embedding shifts by this magnitude---a non-trivial
constraint because the embedding is derived from high-level PE structure,
not raw bytes.

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness of the Behavioural Agent}
For the Behavioural Agent, the minimal counterfactual perturbation
to LBA and GPA variance features is 0.9\%--2.5\% relative
change~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa}.
Although individually small, achieving such precision during active
encryption requires the attacker to implement a memory-access
throttling mechanism that suppresses the statistical signature of
ransomware I/O---a significant engineering cost.

\subsection{Robustness via Uncertainty as a Security Property}
The $\smax$ mechanism provides implicit adversarial
robustness: an adversary who successfully obfuscates one modality
(e.g., crafting a PE binary that moves the semantic embedding toward
the benign region) typically does so at the cost of increased
semantic uncertainty.
This elevated $\sigma_\sem$ prevents AUTO-CONTAIN (Eq.~\eqref{eq:auto})
and escalates the decision to human review, ensuring that uncertain
adversarial samples are not silently allowed.

\subsection{Knowledge Agent Robustness}
The Knowledge Agent operates on discourse features that the adversary
cannot directly influence at the binary level.
A new ransomware campaign can suppress its ATT\&CK tactic signature
only by avoiding the canonical deployment chain (no lateral movement,
no exfiltration tools, no established C2), which is operationally
difficult for enterprise ransomware targeting large networks.
The Knowledge Agent thus provides an adversarially hard complement
to the statistical agents.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Escalation rate reduction.}
The 9\% escalation rate under the low-escalation policy, compared to
up to 22\% in the two-agent baseline, demonstrates the practical
benefit of the Knowledge Agent: discourse features provide a
strong additional signal that resolves ambiguous cases before
they reach the analyst queue.

\textbf{Uncertainty hierarchy.}
The $\bar{\sigma}$ progression ($\approx 0 \to 0.014 \to 0.050$)
is interpretable and expected: static PE structure is the most
deterministic predictor, runtime telemetry introduces moderate
overlap, and discourse features---derived from a small,
heuristically labelled dataset---carry the most uncertainty.
This hierarchy informs the assignment of $\kappa_{\mathrm{local}}$
thresholds: the Semantic Agent rarely consults, while the Knowledge
Agent consults approximately 38\% of the time.

\textbf{RansKnow dataset scale.}
The 307-sample RansKnow is admittedly small.
The heuristic labelling (tactic/tool count thresholds) may not
generalise to all discourse styles.
Future work should extend RansKnow with expert-annotated samples and
include structured threat-intelligence feeds (TAXII/STIX) as
additional feature sources.

\textbf{Fusion without a unified dataset.}
The current evaluation cannot measure true tri-modal fusion because
no dataset provides simultaneous PE, runtime, and discourse features
for the same executable.
Constructing such a dataset is an important future direction.

%%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Dataset independence.}
RDset, RanSMAP, and RansKnow cover distinct samples;
a unified tri-modal dataset would enable true end-to-end fusion evaluation.

\item \textbf{RansKnow scale and labelling.}
307~samples with heuristic labels is the smallest benchmark
in this study.
Results should be interpreted with appropriate statistical caution.

\item \textbf{Semantic agent saturation.}
AUC~1.0 on RDset may reflect dataset saturation rather than
a universal capability.
Performance on out-of-distribution families is not measured here.

\item \textbf{Consultation threshold tuning.}
Per-agent $\kappa_{\mathrm{local}}$ thresholds are currently fixed;
joint Bayesian optimisation over all seven policy hyperparameters
($\tau, \kappa, \tauh, \klow, \kappa_{\mathrm{local},\sem},
\kappa_{\mathrm{local},\beh}, \kappa_{\mathrm{local},\know}$)
is left for future work.

\item \textbf{Expert Feedback Loop evaluation.}
Due to the absence of a longitudinal deployment environment,
the feedback loop is described and formalised but not empirically
evaluated in this study.
\end{enumerate}

%%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \textbf{Extended Agentic SABRE (SABRE+)},
a three-agent uncertainty-aware framework for adaptive ransomware
detection that advances the two-agent SABRE baseline through
three principled contributions.
The Knowledge Agent, trained on the novel RansKnow dataset of
307 YouTube-sourced practitioner discourse samples, achieves 0.9550
accuracy and 0.9950~AUC from 23 structured ATT\&CK and tool features,
providing a modality-orthogonal signal that complements both static and
runtime representations.
The Peer Consultation Protocol enables agents under high epistemic
uncertainty to update their probability estimates using
inverse-uncertainty-weighted peer scores, improving Knowledge Agent
accuracy by +0.005 at a 38\% consultation rate while leaving
uncertainty values unchanged.
The Expert Feedback Loop formalises analyst-correction-driven
incremental retraining of the Decision MLP with experience replay,
providing a principled path to continual adaptation without
catastrophic forgetting.
Together, these contributions reduce the escalation rate to 9\%
under the low-escalation policy---a 59\% relative reduction versus
the two-agent baseline---while maintaining perfect discrimination on the
semantic modality and retaining the full security properties of the
triage architecture.
