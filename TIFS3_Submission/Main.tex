\begin{abstract}
Ransomware detection demands more than a binary verdict: it requires
uncertainty estimation, heterogeneous evidence fusion, and
uncertainty-driven triage. We present a three-agent framework with four
contributions. First, a Semantic CNN (static PE embeddings), a
Behavioural CNN (16D hypervisor telemetry), and a Knowledge CNN (ATT\&CK
tactic embeddings) each use Monte Carlo Dropout for predictive
probability and epistemic uncertainty. Second, a Decision MLP learns a
temperature-calibrated risk score (ECE $0.027$) from the six-dimensional
score pool; since the Semantic and Knowledge Agents contribute only a
fixed corpus-mean score during RanSMAP fusion, naive mean fusion is
better-calibrated still (ECE $0.017$), so fusion's value lies in the
uncertainty structure it exposes for triage rather than a calibration or
AUROC gain; CTGAN augmentation addresses score-space imbalance without
retraining base agents. Third, a four-threshold triage policy optimised
via Bayesian TPE cuts escalation from $58.3\%$ to $30.6\%$ while raising
balanced accuracy from $0.753$ to $0.864$. Fourth, Peer Consultation mitigates
knowledge collapse under concurrent execution, and an Expert Feedback Loop
with Elastic Weight Consolidation limits catastrophic forgetting
($\Delta\text{AUPRC}{=}0.001$). Over six seeds and four generalisation splits on
RanSMAP, RDset, and RansKnow, the framework achieves AUROC $0.996$ (static
PE) and $0.963$ (behavioural), matched by the fused Decision MLP ($0.952$).
Family holdout AUROC falls to $0.707$ ($0.681$ fused): epistemic uncertainty
routes most novel-family samples to escalation rather than silent miss,
though Ryuk is an exception where the system is confidently wrong. A
Mondrian conformal prediction wrapper adds distribution-free coverage
under exchangeability, auto-classifying $55\%$ of traffic at $99\%$ coverage.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Ransomware detection, multi-agent systems, uncertainty quantification,
generative adversarial networks, continual learning, conformal prediction.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Ransomware attacks impose escalating operational and financial costs on
enterprises and critical infrastructure~\cite{beaman2021ransomware}.
State-of-the-art detectors---signature-based antivirus, EDR platforms, or
machine-learning classifiers on file-level features---converge on a common
architectural limitation: a single scalar confidence score mapped to a
binary verdict. When a novel variant or obfuscated execution degrades that
score silently, no signal informs the operator that the verdict is
unreliable.

We identify three structural gaps in the current state of practice:
\textbf{no principled uncertainty}, since a softmax output from a
deterministic network is a point estimate that cannot distinguish a
confident correct prediction from a confident incorrect one;
\textbf{heuristic modality fusion}, since equal-weight averaging of
static PE features and runtime telemetry discards the per-modality
reliability signal that a semantic agent on encrypted PE sections and a
behavioural agent on obfuscated I/O patterns should not share equally;
and \textbf{binary triage}, since an alert on every uncertain detection
overwhelms analysts while routing every uncertain detection to automatic
containment risks business disruption.

This paper presents a framework addressing all three gaps, treating
uncertainty as a \emph{control variable} for deployment rather than a
diagnostic afterthought, via the four contributions below.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{heterogeneous three-agent architecture} in which a Semantic
        CNN, a Behavioural CNN, and a Knowledge CNN each produce a predictive
        probability and an epistemic uncertainty via $T=10$ MC Dropout forward
        passes.
  \item A \textbf{Decision MLP fusion mechanism} with temperature calibration
        (ECE $0.027 \pm 0.003$) that implicitly down-weights the Knowledge
        Agent under elevated uncertainty; because the Semantic and Knowledge
        Agents contribute a fixed corpus-mean score for RanSMAP samples,
        naive mean fusion over the same pool is in fact better-calibrated
        (ECE $0.017$), so its principal value lies in the uncertainty
        structure it exposes for the triage policy
        (Section~\ref{sec:results_tpe}) rather than a calibration or AUROC
        advantage, supported by CTGAN score-space augmentation to handle
        class imbalance at the fusion layer.
  \item A \textbf{formal triage policy} over $(\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max})$ space
        with four jointly optimised thresholds via Bayesian TPE, reducing
        escalation rate from $58.3\%$ to $30.6\%$ while improving balanced accuracy
        from $0.753$ to $0.864$.
  \item A \textbf{Peer Consultation} mechanism that prevents knowledge collapse
        under concurrent execution, and an \textbf{Expert Feedback Loop} with
        EWC enabling continual family adaptation without catastrophic
        forgetting.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Behavioural Ransomware Detection}

Hypervisor-level behavioural monitoring~\cite{HiranoK22,hirano2025ransmap}
captures low-level I/O and memory access patterns before encryption
artefacts surface in the filesystem, providing strong coverage of novel
variants, though IRP monitors~\cite{scaife_cryptolock_2016} remain
vulnerable to slow-encrypt evasion and API-call
classifiers~\cite{sgandurra_automated_2016} require
costly process-level instrumentation.
Survey work~\cite{moussaileb2021survey} confirms that
runtime behavioural methods outperform static analysis against obfuscated
ransomware, but notes a structural limitation shared by all existing
systems: they produce a scalar confidence score with no mechanism for
expressing per-sample epistemic uncertainty or routing uncertain
detections to analyst escalation.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification in Detection}

Monte Carlo Dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout} approximates a posterior over
network weights via repeated stochastic forward passes at test time, at
$T\times$ inference cost (formalised in Section~\ref{sec:agents}).
Gawlikowski et al.~\cite{gawlikowski2023Uncertainity} establish MC Dropout
and deep ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan_simple_2017} as the most broadly
applicable of four neural-network uncertainty method families, though
ensembles require retaining $M$ full model copies at inference.
For distribution-free guarantees, conformal prediction~\cite{papadopoulos_icp_2002}
and its Mondrian variant~\cite{vovk_mondrian_2003} bound coverage
probability without parametric assumptions, generalised to arbitrary risk
functions by Angelopoulos and Bates~\cite{angelopoulos_conformal_risk_2022}.
Uncertainty estimation has been applied to network anomaly
detection~\cite{Doan2024uncertainBay}, but ransomware \emph{triage}---where
an autonomous-containment error differs qualitatively in cost from an
escalation---has not previously been framed as an uncertainty-conditioned
routing problem.

\subsection{Multi-Agent and Ensemble Detection}

Ensemble methods for malware classification~\cite{sagi_ensemble_2018,
masum2022ransomware} aggregate multiple classifiers over a shared feature
space via majority vote or average-score fusion.
Multi-agent intrusion detection systems~\cite{MultiAgentAdaptiveDetection2023}
distribute detection across specialised agents monitoring different network
zones, combined through a centralised coordinator.
Multi-modal ransomware detection combining static PE features, API-call
sequences, and file-system events~\cite{moussaileb2021survey} typically
uses equal-weight or attention-based fusion that does not propagate
per-modality reliability.

Our framework differs structurally in two respects: the three agents
operate on \emph{structurally heterogeneous} input spaces ($\R^{384}$ PE
embeddings, $\R^{16}$ runtime telemetry, $\R^{23}$ ATT\&CK vectors) that
cannot be concatenated without discarding modality-specific structure, and
each agent contributes not only a probability but an \emph{epistemic
uncertainty}, which the Decision MLP uses implicitly to down-weight
unreliable modalities and which the orchestrator uses explicitly to route
uncertain cases to analyst escalation---a mechanism absent from standard
ensemble methods.

\subsection{Generative Augmentation and Continual Learning}

Class imbalance in detection datasets is routinely addressed by
SMOTE~\cite{chawla2002smote} and Conditional Tabular GAN
(CTGAN)~\cite{xu_ctgan_2019}, applied to intrusion
detection~\cite{lin_idsgan_2022} and consistently outperforming SMOTE
when the minority class is small and non-Gaussian.
We apply CTGAN at the \emph{score space} output of the multi-agent
detector: operating on bounded $[0,1]^6$ probability--uncertainty vectors
constrains synthetic samples to the agents' output range, reducing the
risk of implausible combinations and eliminating raw-telemetry
reconstruction artefacts.

Catastrophic forgetting---gradient updates for a new task systematically
overwriting prior weight configurations~\cite{kirkpatrick_ewc_2017}---is
the key barrier to continual adaptation; TESSERACT~\cite{pendlebury_tesseract_2019}
establishes that standard classifiers lose substantial accuracy within
months of the training cutoff under concept drift.
Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick_ewc_2017} mitigates
this via a diagonal Fisher penalty protecting parameters important to
retained tasks; our Expert Feedback Loop applies EWC to the Decision MLP
with negligible retained-family forgetting after repeated correction
iterations (Section~\ref{sec:results_ewc}), enabling continual adaptation
without retraining base agents.

\subsection{Relationship to Prior Work}

This paper extends the two-agent Agentic SABRE
framework~\cite{kabuye2026sabre} (Semantic + Behavioural CNN, MC
Dropout, fixed triage policy) with the four contributions listed
above---a third Knowledge agent, Bayesian TPE threshold optimisation,
CTGAN score-space augmentation, and EWC/conformal wrappers---each
technically distinct from that prior work. All experiments and reported
numerical results in this paper were rerun independently; no numerical
results or figures are reused from~\cite{kabuye2026sabre}, though both
papers draw on the same underlying RDset and RanSMAP datasets.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Detection Task}

Let $\mathcal{X} = \mathcal{X}_{\text{sem}} \times \mathcal{X}_{\text{beh}}
\times \mathcal{X}_{\text{know}}$ denote the joint input space of three
heterogeneous modalities, and let $\mathcal{Y} = \{0,1\}$ denote the binary
ransomware label. A sample is observed as a triple
$\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}_{\text{sem}}, \mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}},
\mathbf{x}_{\text{know}}) \in \mathcal{X}$, where

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf{x}_{\text{sem}} \in \R^{384}$ is a sentence-transformer
        embedding of Portable Executable (PE) section names;
  \item $\mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}} \in \R^{16}$ is a 16-dimensional feature
        vector extracted from a $T_{\text{window}}=10\,\text{s}$ telemetry
        window (see Section~\ref{sec:agents});
  \item $\mathbf{x}_{\text{know}} \in \R^{d}$ is an embedding of
        ATT\&CK tactic profiles derived from threat-intelligence transcripts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Triage Task}

Rather than mapping $\mathbf{x}$ to a binary label, the triage task maps
$\mathbf{x}$ to one of three actions $\mathcal{A} =
\{\textsc{Auto-Contain},\, \textsc{Escalate},\, \textsc{Allow}\}$ according
to a policy $\pi : (\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max}) \to \mathcal{A}$, where
$\hat{p} \in [0,1]$ is the fused risk score and $\sigma_{\max} \in [0,1]$ is
the maximum per-agent epistemic uncertainty.

\begin{definition}[Triage Policy]\label{def:policy}
Given thresholds $\Theta = (\tau_{\text{high}}, \tau, \kappa,
\kappa_{\text{low}})$ with $0 < \tau \leq \tau_{\text{high}} \leq 1$ and
$0 < \kappa_{\text{low}} \leq \kappa \leq 1$, the triage policy is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:policy}
  \pi(\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max}; \Theta) =
  \begin{cases}
    \textsc{Auto-Contain} & \hat{p} \geq \tau_{\text{high}}
                            \;\wedge\; \sigma_{\max} \leq \kappa_{\text{low}}, \\
    \textsc{Escalate}     & \hat{p} \geq \tau
                            \;\vee\; \sigma_{\max} > \kappa, \\
    \textsc{Allow}        & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Since $\tau \leq \tau_{\text{high}}$, the \textsc{Auto-Contain} and
\textsc{Escalate} conditions overlap on the region $\hat{p} \geq
\tau_{\text{high}} \wedge \sigma_{\max} \leq \kappa_{\text{low}}$: this is
not an ambiguity but a stated precedence order, evaluated top-to-bottom, so
\textsc{Auto-Contain} takes priority whenever both conditions hold.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Optimisation Objective}

Let $\text{bal\_acc}(\Theta)$ denote the balanced accuracy of the joint
detection--triage system under thresholds $\Theta$, and let
$\text{esc\_rate}(\Theta)$ denote the fraction of samples routed to
\textsc{Escalate}. The policy optimisation problem is:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
  \Theta^* = \argmax_{\Theta \in \mathcal{F}} \;
  J(\Theta) \coloneqq \text{bal\_acc}(\Theta)
  - \lambda\,\text{esc\_rate}(\Theta),
\end{equation}

where $\lambda \geq 0$ is an operational cost parameter encoding the relative
burden of analyst escalation, and $\mathcal{F}$ is the feasibility region
$\{\Theta : \tau \leq \tau_{\text{high}},\; \kappa_{\text{low}} \leq
\kappa\}$.

\begin{remark}
The parameter $\lambda$ is a deployment decision, not a modelling one. An
organisation with constrained analyst capacity sets higher $\lambda$; one
prioritising missed-detection avoidance sets $\lambda \approx 0$.
\end{remark}

\section{Agent Architecture and Uncertainty Quantification}\label{sec:agents}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Monte Carlo Dropout as Approximate Bayesian Inference}

Let $f_\theta : \mathcal{X}_i \to [0,1]$ denote a neural network with
parameters $\theta$ trained on modality $i$; standard dropout is disabled
at inference, yielding a deterministic point estimate. MC
Dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout} instead keeps dropout \emph{active} at test
time, treating each stochastic forward pass as a draw from an approximate
posterior $q_\phi(\theta) \approx p(\theta \mid \mathcal{D})$.

Formally, for $T$ stochastic forward passes with dropout masks
$\{\mathbf{z}_t\}_{t=1}^T$:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:mc_mean}
  \hat{p}_i(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T}
    f_{\theta \odot \mathbf{z}_t}(\mathbf{x}),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:mc_std}
  \sigma_i(\mathbf{x}) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T-1} \sum_{t=1}^{T}
    \left(f_{\theta \odot \mathbf{z}_t}(\mathbf{x}) - \hat{p}_i(\mathbf{x})
    \right)^2},
\end{equation}

where $\odot$ denotes element-wise masking. The quantity $\sigma_i$ captures
\emph{epistemic} uncertainty---reducible uncertainty arising from limited
training data---as distinct from \emph{aleatoric} uncertainty inherent to the
data generating process~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties}.

\begin{proposition}[Epistemic Signal]\label{prop:epistemic}
When a test sample $\mathbf{x}$ lies far from the training manifold of agent
$i$ (i.e., a novel ransomware family), we hypothesise that the variance
$\sigma_i^2(\mathbf{x})$ tends to increase with distributional shift
$d(\mathbf{x}, \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}})$~\cite{gal2016dropout}.
Approximate Bayesian methods do not provide a formal monotonicity guarantee
in general; we verify this tendency empirically across family and hardware
holdout splits (Supplementary Section~S-VI).
\end{proposition}

We use $T=10$ passes per agent; a T-sweep against a near-converged $T{=}200$
reference (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) shows $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ reaches
within $11\%$ of that reference by $T{=}10$, down from $100\%$ at $T{=}1$,
at $1.9\,\text{ms}$ latency versus $9.5\,\text{ms}$ at $T{=}50$ (which itself
is only within $2\%$ of the reference). Supplementary Section~S-VI
quantifies this stability and the associated latency trade-off.

\subsection{Semantic Agent}

% ── Architecture ──
The Semantic Agent operates on static PE structure. Given a PE file, section
names are concatenated and encoded via a frozen sentence transformer
(all-MiniLM-L6-v2) to produce $\mathbf{x}_{\text{sem}} \in \R^{384}$.
A Semantic CNN $f_{\text{sem}}$ with MC Dropout maps this embedding to
$(\hat{p}_{\text{sem}}, \sigma_{\text{sem}})$.

% ── Training data ──
Training data: RDset~\cite{kabuye2025explainable,mathur2020ransomware}
(Section~\ref{sec:datasets}), with SMOTE balancing applied at the feature
level prior to training.

\subsection{Behavioural Agent}

% ── Feature vector ──
The Behavioural Agent operates on runtime storage and memory access patterns
collected via a thin BitVisor hypervisor~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}. Within
each $T_{\text{window}}=10\,\text{s}$ window we compute a
$\mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}} \in \R^{16}$ feature vector partitioned as
$\mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}} = (\chi_s, \chi_m)$, where:

\textbf{Storage features} $\chi_s \in \R^{5}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:storage_features}
  \chi_s = \Bigl(
    \bar{T}_{s,r},\;
    \bar{T}_{s,w},\;
    V_{s,r},\;
    V_{s,w},\;
    \bar{H}_{s,w}
  \Bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{T}_{s,r}$ and $\bar{T}_{s,w}$ are mean read/write throughputs
$[\text{byte/s}]$; $V_{s,r}$ and $V_{s,w}$ are variances of accessed Logical
Block Addresses (LBA); and $\bar{H}_{s,w}$ is mean Shannon entropy of written
sectors, computed as $H(X) = -\sum_i p(x_i) \log_2 p(x_i)$.

\textbf{Memory features} $\chi_m \in \R^{11}$:
\begin{align}\label{eq:memory_features}
  \chi_m = \Bigl(
    &H_{m,w},\; H_{m,rw},\;
     C_{4K,r},\; C_{4K,w},\; C_{4K,rw},\; C_{4K,x},\nonumber\\
    &C_{2M,r},\; C_{2M,rw},\;
     C_{\text{MMIO},r},\; C_{\text{MMIO},w},\;
     V_{m,w}
  \Bigr),
\end{align}
where $H_{m,w}$ and $H_{m,rw}$ are Shannon entropies of written and
read/written memory pages; $C_{s,o}$ denotes the count of memory accesses of
size $s \in \{4\,\text{KiB},\,2\,\text{MiB},\,\text{MMIO}\}$ and operation
$o \in \{r,w,rw,x\}$; and $V_{m,w}$ is the variance of written Guest Physical
Addresses (GPA).

% ── Training data ──
Training data: RanSMAP~\cite{hirano2025ransmap} (Section~\ref{sec:datasets}).

\subsection{Knowledge Agent}

% ── Architecture ──
The Knowledge Agent encodes ATT\&CK tactic profiles derived from
threat-intelligence transcripts (RansKnow~\cite{ransknow_2026}: $718$
ATT\&CK-aligned transcripts). A Discourse CNN $f_{\text{know}}$ maps the
tactic embedding $\mathbf{x}_{\text{know}} \in \R^{d}$ to
$(\hat{p}_{\text{know}}, \sigma_{\text{know}})$.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Generalisation Splits}

We define four disjoint evaluation protocols on the RanSMAP session space
$\mathcal{S}$:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Random:} 80/20 stratified split over $\mathcal{S}$.
  \item \textbf{Family holdout} (more precisely a \emph{class} holdout: the
        held-out set spans both ransomware families and benign
        applications)\textbf{:} $\mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} =
        \{s \in \mathcal{S} : \text{family}(s) \in
        \{\text{REvil},\text{WannaCry},\text{Firefox},\text{Zip}\}\}$. We
        retain the shorter name "family holdout" throughout for brevity.
  \item \textbf{Hardware holdout:} $\mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} =
        \{s \in \mathcal{S} : \text{hw}(s) \in
        \{\text{i5-gen12},\text{i7-gen12},\text{i3-gen12/DDR5}\}\}$.
  \item \textbf{Temporal:} $\mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} =
        \{s \in \mathcal{S} : t(s) \geq t_{0.85}\}$,
        where $t_{0.85}$ is the 85th percentile timestamp.
\end{enumerate}

All results are reported as mean $\pm$ std over $K=6$ independent random
seeds.

\section{Decision MLP Fusion and Score-Space Augmentation}\label{sec:fusion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Score Pool Construction}

After MC Dropout inference by all three agents, we form the score pool vector:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score_pool}
  \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}) = \bigl(
    \hat{p}_{\text{sem}},\; \sigma_{\text{sem}},\;
    \hat{p}_{\text{beh}},\; \sigma_{\text{beh}},\;
    \hat{p}_{\text{know}},\; \sigma_{\text{know}}
  \bigr) \in [0,1]^6.
\end{equation}
The maximum per-agent uncertainty $\sigma_{\max} =
\max(\sigma_{\text{sem}},\sigma_{\text{beh}},\sigma_{\text{know}})$
is derived from $\mathbf{v}$ and used independently in the triage
policy (Section~\ref{sec:triage}), but is not fed to the Decision MLP.

\begin{remark}[Cross-Dataset Fusion Without Per-Sample Pairing]\label{rem:corpus_mean}
RDset, RanSMAP, and RansKnow are independently collected: no executable
appears in more than one dataset, so a RanSMAP execution window has no
corresponding PE binary or ATT\&CK transcript. We therefore do not attempt
to condition $\hat{p}_{\text{sem}}$ or $\hat{p}_{\text{know}}$ on the
RanSMAP sample's own (family) identity---doing so via a per-family lookup
keyed by the sample's true label would leak that label into the score pool.
Instead, for every RanSMAP evaluation, $(\hat{p}_{\text{sem}},
\sigma_{\text{sem}})$ and $(\hat{p}_{\text{know}}, \sigma_{\text{know}})$ are
fixed, label-independent constants: the MC Dropout mean and standard
deviation of each agent's own corpus (RDset for Semantic, RansKnow for
Knowledge), computed once and reused for every RanSMAP sample regardless of
family. Consequently, in the RanSMAP evaluations of
Section~\ref{sec:eval}, only the Behavioural Agent carries per-sample
discriminative signal; the Semantic and Knowledge Agents contribute a fixed
prior that the Decision MLP can learn to weight, and Peer Consultation
(Section~\ref{sec:feedback}) can still substitute when
$\sigma_{\text{know}}$ or $\sigma_{\text{sem}}$ exceeds a peer's, but neither
agent can improve fused AUROC over the Behavioural Agent alone in this
setting. This is why the Decision MLP's contribution below is measured in
calibration and uncertainty-gated triage quality rather than in raw
discrimination. Table~\ref{tab:agent_dataset_map} makes this mapping
explicit.

\begin{remark}[Why Score Space]
Fusing in score space---rather than feature space---preserves the
per-modality uncertainty structure. Feature-space concatenation of
$\R^{384} \oplus \R^{16} \oplus \R^d$ would conflate structurally
incompatible representations and preclude independent uncertainty propagation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{CTGAN Score-Space Augmentation}

Let $\mathcal{V}_{\text{train}} = \{(\mathbf{v}_j, y_j)\}_{j=1}^{N}$ denote
the score-pool training set with labels $y_j \in \{0,1\}$, where the
ransomware class is a fraction $r \ll 0.5$ of samples. We apply Conditional
Tabular GAN (CTGAN)~\cite{xu_ctgan_2019} to generate synthetic minority-class
score-pool vectors.

The CTGAN minimax objective is:
\begin{align}\label{eq:ctgan}
  \min_G \max_D \;
  &\E_{\mathbf{v} \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(\mathbf{v})] \nonumber\\
  +\; &\E_{\mathbf{z} \sim p_z,\, c \sim p_c}[\log(1-D(G(\mathbf{z},c)))]
  - \gamma \Vert \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{v}}} D(\hat{\mathbf{v}}) \Vert^2,
\end{align}
where $c$ is a conditional class label, $\mathbf{z}$ is a latent noise vector,
$\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ is a linear interpolation between real and generated
samples, and $\gamma$ is the gradient-penalty coefficient.

Crucially, CTGAN operates on $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^6$, not on raw features:
this ensures that the generator cannot produce physically impossible score
combinations and preserves the calibration structure of the base agents.

\subsection{Decision MLP Architecture}

The Decision MLP $g_\phi : [0,1]^6 \to [0,1]$ maps the score pool to a
fused risk score:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mlp}
  g_\phi(\mathbf{v}) = \sigma\!\left(
    \mathbf{W}_2 \,\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{b}_1)
    + \mathbf{b}_2
  \right),
\end{equation}
with dimensions $6 \to 32 \to 1$ and sigmoid output. Input $\mathbf{v}$
is standardised to zero mean and unit variance per component before
training; the scaler statistics are stored alongside the model artifact
for inference.

\subsection{Temperature Calibration}

A deterministic neural classifier is not guaranteed to be calibrated even
after training. We apply temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration}: the
logit $\ell = \log(\hat{p}/(1-\hat{p}))$ is divided by a learned scalar
$T > 0$ before the final sigmoid:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:temperature}
  \hat{p}_{\text{cal}} = \sigma(\ell / T),
  \quad T^* = \argmin_T \; \mathrm{NLL}_T(\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}).
\end{equation}

Expected Calibration Error is defined as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ece}
  \ECE = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \frac{|B_m|}{N}
    \left| \text{acc}(B_m) - \text{conf}(B_m) \right|,
\end{equation}
where $\{B_m\}_{m=1}^M$ partition $[0,1]$ into $M$ equal-width confidence
bins, $\text{acc}(B_m)$ is empirical accuracy and $\text{conf}(B_m)$ is mean
predicted confidence within bin $B_m$.

The mean fusion baseline ($\text{ECE}=0.017$) is in fact better-calibrated
than the Decision MLP with temperature scaling ($\text{ECE}=0.027$)
(Section~\ref{sec:results_fusion}).

\section{Triage Policy and Bayesian Optimisation}\label{sec:triage}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Triage Policy in $(\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max})$ Space}

The triage policy $\pi$ (Definition~\ref{def:policy}) partitions the unit
square $[0,1]^2$ into three regions. The fused risk $\hat{p} =
g_\phi(\mathbf{v})$ and maximum agent uncertainty $\sigma_{\max}$ jointly
determine the action. The boundary conditions ensure that:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{Auto-Contain} requires \emph{both} high risk \emph{and} low
        uncertainty---autonomous action is taken only when the system is
        jointly confident in detection and reliable;
  \item \textsc{Escalate} fires when \emph{either} the risk score is elevated
        ($\hat{p} \geq \tau$) \emph{or} agent disagreement is high
        ($\sigma_{\max} > \kappa$), routing borderline and uncertain samples to
        an analyst rather than acting autonomously;
  \item \textsc{Allow} covers the confident-benign region.
\end{itemize}

The three regions are illustrated geometrically, with the distribution of
training-distribution ransomware, benign, and OOD samples in
$(\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max})$ space, in Supplementary Section~S-X: OOD
samples concentrate in the \textsc{Escalate} region ($63\%$) due to
elevated $\sigma_{\max}$, though not exclusively ($19\%$ still reach
\textsc{Auto-Contain}).

\subsection{Bayesian Triage Policy Optimisation via TPE}

The objective $J(\Theta)$ in~\eqref{eq:objective} is non-differentiable in
$\Theta$ (discrete triage counts), so we employ Bayesian optimisation via
Tree-structured Parzen Estimation (TPE)~\cite{bergstra_algorithms_2011}.

TPE models the probability density of $J$ conditioned on $\Theta$ as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tpe}
  p(\Theta \mid J) =
  \begin{cases}
    \ell(\Theta) & J(\Theta) < J^*, \\
    g(\Theta)    & J(\Theta) \geq J^*,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $J^*$ is the $\gamma$-quantile of observed objective values, $\ell$ is
a Parzen-window density over good configurations, and $g$ is a density over
poor ones. The acquisition criterion selects:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ei}
  \Theta_{\text{next}} = \argmax_\Theta
  \frac{\ell(\Theta)}{g(\Theta)},
\end{equation}
which approximates the Expected Improvement criterion.

We run $300$ TPE trials over $\mathcal{F}$ with $\lambda = 0.3$; the search
budget is chosen based on the dimensionality of $\Theta$ and the cost of
evaluating $J$ on the validation set (approximately $0.15\,\text{s}$ per
trial). Results and convergence analysis are presented in
Section~\ref{sec:results_tpe}.

\subsection{Comparison with Grid Search and Fixed Thresholds}

Table~\ref{tab:policy_compare} summarises the triage outcome under the
three policies on the random-stratified test split. Following the
RansKnow correction, Bayesian TPE and grid search converge to
statistically indistinguishable balanced accuracy ($0.864$ for both),
with TPE retaining a modest edge in escalation rate ($30.6\%$ vs
$32.6\%$) and auto-contain rate ($28.2\%$ vs $26.2\%$): optimisation
still pays off, but the margin over grid search has narrowed
substantially now that the underlying Knowledge score is more reliable.
Fixed thresholds ($\tau{=}0.10,\,\kappa{=}0.10$, inherited

corrected Knowledge Agent they reach $0.753$ balanced accuracy at

balanced accuracy by $0.112$, roughly halves escalation
($58.3\%\!\to\!30.6\%$), and increases the auto-contain rate by
$1.8\times$ ($16.0\%\!\to\!28.2\%$).

\section{Peer Consultation and Expert Feedback Loop}\label{sec:feedback}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Peer Consultation}

Under concurrent benign--ransomware execution, the Behavioural Agent observes
superimposed access patterns, inflating $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ and
suppressing $\hat{p}_{\text{beh}}$. To prevent \emph{knowledge collapse}
(a single-agent failure cascading to an incorrect triage decision), we
introduce Peer Consultation (PC).

\begin{definition}[Peer Consultation]\label{def:pc}
When agent $i$'s epistemic uncertainty $\sigma_i(\mathbf{x}) > \kappa_{\text{PC}}$
for a threshold $\kappa_{\text{PC}} > 0$, agent $i$'s \emph{probability only}
is replaced by a weighted combination of its peer agents' current
probabilities:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peer_consultation}
  \hat{p}_{i}^{\text{PC}}(\mathbf{x}) =
    \sum_{j \neq i} w_j\,\hat{p}_{j}(\mathbf{x}),
\end{equation}
where weights are proportional to the inverse uncertainty of each peer,
$w_j \propto 1/\sigma_j$, normalised so that $\sum_{j \neq i} w_j = 1$.
Agent $i$'s own uncertainty $\sigma_i$ is \emph{not} modified by this
substitution---it is used unchanged by the triage policy
(Section~\ref{sec:triage}), so a consulted agent still contributes its
original (elevated) uncertainty to $\sigma_{\max}$. Each agent maintains
its own threshold $\kappa_{\text{PC}}^{(i)}$; in practice all are set equal
to the triage threshold $\kappa$ optimised by Bayesian TPE.
\end{definition}

The three conditions are evaluated independently in fixed order
(Semantic $\to$ Behavioural $\to$ Knowledge) using whichever
probabilities are current at that point, so a later substitution can
draw on an earlier agent's already-substituted value rather than its
original---the update is sequential, not simultaneous. Peer Consultation
never suppresses the triggering agent's own uncertainty; it routes to
\textsc{Escalate} while ensuring the score pool reflects the more
reliable modalities' evidence.

\subsection{Expert Feedback Loop with EWC}

The Expert Feedback Loop allows an analyst-approved correction label
$\tilde{y}$ for an escalated sample to update the Decision MLP $g_\phi$
incrementally via Elastic Weight Consolidation
(EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick_ewc_2017}, without catastrophic forgetting of the
base agents' knowledge.
Let $\phi^*$ be the parameters of $g_\phi$ trained on the original dataset
$\mathcal{D}_0$, and let $F_j$ denote the diagonal Fisher information for
parameter $j$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fisher}
  F_j = \E_{\mathbf{v} \sim \mathcal{D}_0}\!\left[
    \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial \phi_j}
    \log p(y \mid \mathbf{v}; \phi^*)\right)^2
  \right].
\end{equation}

The EWC loss for the incremental update on correction set
$\mathcal{D}_{\text{new}}$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ewc}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{EWC}}(\phi) =
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\phi; \mathcal{D}_{\text{new}})
    + \frac{\lambda_{\text{EWC}}}{2}
      \sum_{j} F_j \left(\phi_j - \phi^*_j\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$ is the cross-entropy loss on new samples and
$\lambda_{\text{EWC}}$ controls the trade-off between plasticity and
stability. We measure catastrophic forgetting as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forgetting}
  \Delta\text{AUPRC} =
    \text{AUPRC}(\phi; \mathcal{D}_0) -
    \text{AUPRC}(\phi_{\text{new}}; \mathcal{D}_0),
\end{equation}
achieving $\Delta\text{AUPRC} = 0.001$ on retained families after five
feedback iterations.

\section{Conformal Prediction Wrapper}\label{sec:conformal}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Mondrian Conformal Prediction}

To provide distribution-free coverage guarantees on triage outcomes, we wrap
the fused output $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}}$ in a Mondrian conformal predictor.
Let $\alpha \in (0,1)$ be the target miscoverage rate.

\begin{definition}[Conformal Prediction Set]\label{def:cp}
Given a calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(\mathbf{v}_i,
y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$, define non-conformity scores $s_i =
1 - f_y(\mathbf{v}_i)$ for the true class $y_i$. The conformal prediction
set at level $\alpha$ for a new sample $\mathbf{v}$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp_set}
  \mathcal{C}_\alpha(\mathbf{v}) = \bigl\{y \in \mathcal{Y} :
    s(\mathbf{v}, y) \leq Q_{1-\alpha}(\{s_i\}_{i=1}^n)\bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $Q_{1-\alpha}$ is the $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of calibration scores.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Marginal Coverage Guarantee]\label{thm:coverage}
Under exchangeability of calibration and test samples:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coverage}
  \Prob\!\left(Y \in \mathcal{C}_\alpha(\mathbf{v})\right) \geq 1 - \alpha.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Mondrian conformal prediction stratifies the calibration non-conformity
scores by each calibration sample's \emph{true} label (standard
label-conditional Mondrian CP), conditioning~\eqref{eq:cp_set} separately
for each class; this yields class-conditional coverage empirically but
does not carry a guarantee beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage}'s marginal
bound. The exchangeability assumption holds for the random-stratified
split; under temporal and family holdout it does not, and coverage is
verified empirically instead (Section~\ref{sec:results_conformal}).

\section{Experimental Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Datasets}\label{sec:datasets}

\textbf{RDset}~\cite{kabuye2025explainable,mathur2020ransomware}:
$138{,}047$ PE files ($41{,}123$ ransomware; $96{,}924$ benign; $\approx\!30\%$
prevalence), used exclusively for Semantic Agent training/evaluation with
disjoint train/test splits at the binary (file-hash) level.

\textbf{RanSMAP}~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}: $35{,}857$ ten-second execution
windows from $1{,}776$ sessions across 15 hardware configurations (four
CPU models, six DDR4/DDR5 frequencies, two RAM capacities), six
ransomware families
(Conti, Darkside, LockBit, REvil, Ryuk, WannaCry), and six benign
applications (AESCrypt, Firefox, Idle, Office, SDelete, Zip) under BitVisor
hypervisor surveillance; moderate class imbalance addressed via SMOTE
at the feature level. A separate \texttt{variants} partition provides
$1{,}397$ windows from seven Conti sub-variants for OOD analysis; a
\texttt{mix} partition provides $600$ windows from ten concurrent
execution combinations. Neither is used for training.

\textbf{RansKnow}~\cite{ransknow_2026}: $718$ ATT\&CK-aligned
threat-intelligence transcripts, used exclusively for Knowledge Agent
training. Score-space imbalance at the fusion layer is addressed
separately by CTGAN augmentation (Section~\ref{sec:fusion}).

\subsection{Implementation Details}

\textbf{Agent architectures.} All three base agents share one lightweight
single-block design, scaled to their respective input dimensionality: a
single-\emph{channel} input sequence (of length $384$, $16$, or $23$
respectively---the input dimensionality, not a channel count) through one
Conv1d($1{\to}C$, $k{=}3$, same-padding)--ReLU--MaxPool1d($2$)--Dropout($p{=}0.3$)
block, Flatten, FC($\to 64$ or $128$)--ReLU, FC($\to 1$)--Sigmoid. The
Semantic CNN takes $\mathbf{x}_{\text{sem}} \in \R^{384}$ with $C{=}32$
channels and a $128$-unit hidden FC layer; SMOTE is applied to RDset at the
embedding level ($r_{\text{minority}}{=}0.30$). The Behavioural CNN takes
$\mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}} \in \R^{16}$ with $C{=}16$ channels and a $64$-unit
hidden FC layer ($8{,}385$ parameters, $32.8\,\text{KB}$); SMOTE is applied
to RanSMAP training windows. The Knowledge CNN mirrors this architecture
scaled to $\mathbf{x}_{\text{know}} \in \R^{23}$, trained on $718$ RansKnow
transcripts (70/15/15 train/val/test, stratified on the binary
Ransomware\_Context label since Family\_List is unavailable for over half
the corpus); SMOTE is applied to the training split post-scaling, as for
the other two agents. None of the three base agents use BatchNorm. The Decision MLP maps
$\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^6$ (standardised per component) through
FC($6{\to}32$)--ReLU--Dropout($p{=}0.2$), FC($32{\to}1$), Sigmoid, with
temperature scaling applied post-hoc via L-BFGS on the validation set.
Full architecture pseudocode is given in Supplementary Algorithms S1--S2.

\textbf{Training and hyperparameters.} All networks use Adam
($\eta{=}10^{-3}$, batch size $64$, up to $50$ epochs, early stopping
patience $6$, best weights restored), $K{=}6$ seeds for all RanSMAP
experiments. CTGAN, Bayesian TPE, and EWC hyperparameters are given with
their respective results in Sections~\ref{sec:results_tpe}
and~\ref{sec:results_ewc} and Supplementary Algorithm S2
(\texttt{ctgan} v0.9, Optuna 3.5). Hardware: NVIDIA GB10 GPU, Python
3.10, PyTorch 2.2; inference latency measured over $500$ repetitions
($50$ warm-up, batch size $32$).

\textbf{Out-of-fold Decision MLP training features.} Scoring the
Behavioural Agent's own training windows with a checkpoint trained on those
same windows would let the Decision MLP fit an optimistically confident
score it never sees again at test time. We therefore build the Behavioural
component of every Decision MLP training vector out-of-fold: the
random-stratified training sessions are partitioned into five
session-grouped, stratified folds (\texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold}), a
Behavioural CNN is trained on each set of four folds, and the held-out
fifth fold is scored with that fold's own checkpoint; concatenating all
five folds' held-out scores covers the full training set with no sample
scored by a model that trained on it. The leave-one-family-out evaluation
(Section~\ref{sec:results_lofo}) applies the same five-fold cross-fitting
\emph{within} each family-excluded training pool, so its Decision MLP
training features are simultaneously family-excluded and out-of-fold; test-time
scoring for each held-out family uses a Behavioural checkpoint trained on
that family-excluded pool, never on the held-out family. Test-time
inference throughout uses the full-training-set checkpoint on genuinely
held-out test sessions and requires no cross-fitting.

(Section~\ref{sec:results_tpe}) depends on.

\subsection{Generalisation Analysis}

Over $K{=}6$ seeds (variability ${<}0.002$ for the Decision MLP on
random, temporal, and hardware), temporal ($0.966$) holdout slightly
exceeds, and hardware ($0.944$) holdout slightly trails, random-stratified
performance ($0.963$), confirming the Behavioural Agent's discrimination
is stable across sessions and hardware without retraining; the Decision
MLP tracks the same pattern a small, consistent margin below it
throughout (Remark~\ref{rem:corpus_mean}), family holdout remaining the
one outlier (Section~\ref{sec:results_agents}). A direct comparison with
Hirano and Kobayashi~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}'s detector on the same
hardware-holdout partition is provided in Section~\ref{sec:discussion_hirano}.

The generalisation profile across all four splits---AUROC, selective
precision-coverage (AUSPC), session-level detection rate, and Mondrian
conformal coverage at $\alpha{=}0.01$---is plotted as a radar chart in
Supplementary Section~S-VIII: random-stratified, temporal, and hardware
holdout cluster tightly near the outer ring on every axis, while family
holdout collapses inward on all four, characterising the same
generalisation gap across discrimination, selective prediction,
deployment-level detection, and formal coverage guarantees.

\subsection{Statistical Significance, Risk-Coverage, and Session-Level Analysis}\label{sec:results_significance}

\textbf{Significance:} a one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test (six per-seed
Decision MLP AUROCs vs.\ the single, fixed, deployed Behavioural CNN
checkpoint, H$_1$: MLP $>$ B) fails to reject the null on every split
($p{=}1.00$ throughout); the Behavioural CNN exceeds the Decision MLP by
$0.009$--$0.029$ AUROC on all four splits. This is the direct, expected
consequence of Remark~\ref{rem:corpus_mean}: once the Knowledge and
Semantic Agents are restricted to a genuinely label-independent corpus-mean
score, fusion cannot add per-sample discriminative signal over the
Behavioural Agent alone, so its measured benefit is the uncertainty-gated
triage policy it supports (Section~\ref{sec:results_tpe}) rather than
AUROC or calibration (Section~\ref{sec:results_fusion}). A \emph{session-level}
McNemar test on random-stratified (majority-vote aggregation, avoiding the
within-session window correlation that invalidates a window-level test)
finds zero disagreements across all $267$ sessions---the two models now
make identical session-level decisions on every session (the McNemar
statistic is degenerate with zero discordant pairs, so we do not report a
$\chi^2$/$p$-value for this test), despite the small, consistent
per-window AUROC gap above; a window-level McNemar on the same split is
nominally significant ($\chi^2{=}53.7$, $p{<}10^{-4}$) but is not
independence-respecting, for the reason above.

\textbf{Selective precision-coverage:} sweeping the auto-contain threshold
$\tau$ and tracing precision against the fraction of traffic covered
(auto-classified) at each threshold, the Area Under the Selective
Precision--Coverage curve (AUSPC; higher is better---this is a
precision-coverage curve, not the conventional risk-coverage curve where
lower is better) is $0.829$ on random-stratified and $0.823$ on hardware
holdout; temporal ($0.674$) and family ($0.650$) holdout are lower,
consistent with the same moderate-vs-severe shift pattern seen in
Table~\ref{tab:auroc_splits}.

\textbf{Session-level detection and latency:} majority-vote aggregation
(flag if $>50\%$ of windows exceed $\hat{p}_{\text{MLP}}{\geq}0.5$)
achieves near-perfect in-distribution detection (random $100\%$, hardware
$99.4\%$, temporal $100\%$) at low session FPR ($2.2$--$6.7\%$), falling to
$70.7\%$ detection (FPR $17.9\%$) on family holdout; max-score (flag if
\emph{any} window exceeds threshold) reaches $100\%$ detection on three of
four splits but at much higher FPR ($81$--$97\%$), confirming majority-vote
is the more reliable session-level rule under honestly out-of-fold scoring.
Median latency to first detection is $0\,\text{s}$ across all splits; mean
latency rises modestly under distribution shift (random $4.3\,\text{s}$ vs.\
family holdout $7.0\,\text{s}$). Full statistics are in Supplementary
Section~S-VIII.

\subsection{OOD Variants and Concurrent Execution}\label{sec:ood_variants}

The \texttt{variants} partition of RanSMAP provides $1{,}397$ windows from
seven Conti sub-variants (Conti.A--G), none seen in training. Against
Hirano and Kobayashi~\cite{hirano2025ransmap} (mean recall $0.56$, full
23D), our Behavioural CNN alone achieves mean recall $0.63$ (16D), with
Conti.G strongest ($0.89$) and Conti.F an outlier at $0.09$. On five of
the six remaining sub-variants, escalation under the fused $[S{+}B{+}K]$
Decision MLP and the real Bayesian-TPE policy is high ($66$--$85\%$):
$\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ rises on novel patterns, routing to
\textsc{Escalate} rather than silent containment---unlike Hirano and
Kobayashi's detector, which has no such fallback. Conti.F is the
exception and a genuine limitation: both its mean fused probability and
$\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ are low (escalation only $24\%$), meaning the system
is confidently wrong rather than appropriately uncertain on this specific
variant---the uncertainty-driven safety net does not catch every evasive
case. This partial coverage gap is consistent with the pattern already
noted for SDelete+REvil below, and is discussed further in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. The full per-variant table and figure are
provided in Supplementary Section~S-VIII.

The \texttt{mix} partition of RanSMAP provides $1{,}944$ windows from ten
concurrent-execution combinations (ransomware + benign application running
simultaneously), the hardest generalisation challenge in the benchmark.
Under concurrent execution, the Behavioural Agent observes superimposed
I/O and memory patterns, inflating $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ and suppressing
$\hat{p}_{\text{beh}}$ for some, though not all, combinations: the most
adversarial scenario is SDelete+REvil, detecting only $27.1\%$ of the time
at the Behavioural Agent alone and $49.0\%$ in the [B+K] mean fusion
baseline (Remark~\ref{rem:corpus_mean}), as SDelete's legitimate file
deletion I/O overwhelms REvil's signal; SDelete+Conti is the second
hardest ($57.9\%$/$89.3\%$). The remaining eight combinations are detected
$86$--$98\%$ of the time even without Peer Consultation.

Since it is $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ that is elevated on the hard cases, Peer
Consultation (Definition~\ref{def:pc}) triggers on the \emph{Behavioural}
Agent: with PC active, $\hat{p}_{\text{beh}}$ is replaced by the
inverse-uncertainty-weighted average of $\hat{p}_{\text{sem}}$ and
$\hat{p}_{\text{know}}$, recovering the Semantic Agent's partial signal:
detection improves to $50.5\%$ for SDelete+REvil and $90.9\%$ for
SDelete+Conti (from $49.0\%$ and $89.3\%$ without PC). The fixed
corpus-mean Knowledge score is high and stable enough to clear the strict
$\tau_{\text{high}}{=}0.90$ threshold on most mix-partition windows: under
the Bayesian TPE-optimised policy, \textsc{Auto-Contain} fires on
$53.5\%$ of windows without PC and $58.8\%$ with PC, while
\textsc{Escalate} fires on $20$--$83\%$ per combination ($46.5\%$/$41.2\%$
overall). Every window in the mix partition is now escalated or
auto-contained ($0\%$ silently \textsc{Allow}ed), correctly routing
ambiguous cases rather than missing them---unlike Hirano and
Kobayashi~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}'s binary-output detector, which has no
such mechanism. SDelete combinations remain hardest in relative terms
(SDelete+REvil auto-contains only $16.7$--$24.0\%$; SDelete+Conti
$29.4$--$41.6\%$, versus $49$--$81\%$ elsewhere), so autonomous
containment confidence is still weakest here even though the coverage
gap has closed. Per-scenario rates are in Supplementary Section~S-VIII.

\paragraph{Coverage under distribution shift.}
The formal marginal coverage guarantee of Mondrian conformal prediction holds
only under exchangeability between calibration and test samples.
On in-distribution splits (thresholds calibrated on the split-specific
validation set), empirical coverage at $\alpha{=}0.01$ is within
$0.7$ points of the $99\%$ nominal target; family holdout is the critical
exception, collapsing to $\mathbf{88.0\%}$ at $\alpha{=}0.01$ and $59.2\%$

exchangeability is violated when test families are entirely absent from
calibration---consistent

guarantees degrade under covariate shift.

in-distribution validity certificate rather than a universal bound;} for
unseen families, the escalation mechanism (via $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$
thresholding) provides a complementary, uncertainty-driven safety net.

\subsection{Ablation Study}\label{sec:ablation}

Four ablation studies validate key design choices.
\textbf{(1) MC Dropout pass count $T{=}10$:} justified in
Section~\ref{sec:agents} by its convergence/latency trade-off; $T{=}1$
additionally degenerates the triage policy to two actions since
$\sigma_{\text{beh}}{=}0$ by construction with a single pass.
\textbf{(2) Feature ablation:} storage-only ($\chi_s$, 5D) AUROC $0.880$;
memory-only ($\chi_m$, 11D) $0.890$; combined 16D $\mathbf{0.960}$,
confirming complementarity.
\textbf{(3) Decision MLP depth:} one and two hidden-layer variants are
statistically equivalent on AUROC but not calibration
(Section~\ref{sec:results_fusion_compare}); we retain the one-layer
architecture for consistency with the prior two-agent
design~\cite{kabuye2026sabre}.
\textbf{(4) CTGAN ratio:} $N_{\text{synth}} \in [500,5000]$ produces no
significant AUROC/ECE change; we adopt $N_{\text{synth}}{=}2{,}000$.
Full figures and tables are in Supplementary Section~S-VI.

\subsection{Behavioural Baselines, Deep Ensembles, and Knowledge Robustness}\label{sec:results_extra}

Three further checks address questions not covered above (full tables,
methodology, and confusion breakdowns in Supplementary Section~S-IX).
\textbf{Tabular baselines:} on the identical 16D Behavioural feature set,
Random Forest, gradient boosting, and an ordinary MLP all exceed the CNN's
AUROC ($0.963$); we retain the CNN because MC Dropout gives it a native,
integrated uncertainty mechanism the tabular alternatives lack.
\textbf{Deep ensemble vs.\ MC Dropout:} a genuine 6-member ensemble
matches MC Dropout's AUROC with better calibration (ECE $0.027$ vs.\
$0.042$) at $6\times$ the storage cost. \textbf{Knowledge robustness:} a
genuine 2-agent Behavioural+Knowledge fusion is statistically
indistinguishable from Behavioural-alone on AUROC but better calibrated
(ECE $0.018$ vs.\ $0.040$); an adversarial input-swap test shows Peer
Consultation's safeguard can still be evaded by a low-uncertainty
adversarial input, degrading calibration (ECE $0.015{\to}0.023$) though
not AUROC (mechanism in Supplementary Section~S-IX).
\textbf{Cost-sensitive triage:} re-optimising with an illustrative cost
objective (missed ransomware $>$ false auto-contain $>$ escalation)
exposes a tradeoff balanced accuracy alone hides; operators should select
thresholds against their own cost structure (Supplementary
Section~S-IX).

\subsection{Fusion Method Comparison}\label{sec:results_fusion_compare}

We compare the Decision MLP-1L ([S+B+K]) against four alternatives trained
on the identical (unaugmented) random-stratified score pool: na\"{i}ve
[B+K] mean fusion (Semantic excluded, as in Table~\ref{tab:auroc_splits}),
and three further [S+B+K] fusion architectures on the full six-dimensional
pool---Random Forest, Gradient Boosting (GBT), and a two-layer MLP.
On in-distribution splits, all MLP and tree-based methods cluster within
$0.005$ AUROC of each other: GBT and MLP-2L are tied for the highest
AUROC ($0.954$ random-stratified), while GBT is clearly the
best-calibrated (ECE $0.016$), followed by MLP-2L ($0.022$), RF
($0.024$), and MLP-1L ($0.026$). Family holdout degrades all methods
similarly (AUROC $\approx 0.68$), confirming the degradation originates
in the score-pool features rather than the fusion architecture. We retain
the one-layer Decision MLP as the deployed architecture for its
simplicity and continuity with the prior two-agent
design~\cite{kabuye2026sabre}, not for a calibration advantage: on
calibration, once Behavioural scoring is honestly out-of-fold, the
deployed one-layer MLP is in fact the worst-calibrated of the five
methods compared here. Full results are provided in Supplementary
Section~S-VIII.

\subsection{Score-Space Augmentation Under Class Imbalance}\label{sec:results_imbalance}

We evaluate CTGAN score-space augmentation against SMOTE~\cite{chawla2002smote}
under induced class imbalance, subsampling ransomware windows to $10\%$,
$5\%$, and $1\%$ prevalence (benign count fixed at $12{,}225$) and evaluating
on the unmodified random-stratified test split.
AUROC stays above $0.92$ for every method down to $1\%$ ransomware
prevalence ($50{\times}$ fewer positive examples), but calibration degrades
sharply without augmentation as prevalence drops (ECE $0.208$ at $10\%$ to
$0.404$ at $1\%$). CTGAN restores calibration best at $10\%$ prevalence
(ECE $0.070$ vs.\ SMOTE $0.108$), but SMOTE is better at $5\%$ ($0.119$
vs.\ $0.198$) and $1\%$ ($0.136$ vs.\ $0.149$)---the reverse of the trend
we previously reported: CTGAN's relative advantage shrinks rather than
grows as the minority class becomes too sparse ($n{=}123$ at $1\%$) for the
generator to model reliably. Score-space augmentation is thus most
beneficial for calibration rather than AUROC, and SMOTE is the more robust
default under severe score-space imbalance. Full results are provided in
Supplementary Section~S-VIII.

\subsection{Leave-One-Family-Out Robustness}\label{sec:results_lofo}

ransomware families from \emph{both} the Behavioural Agent's training data
and the Decision MLP's training score pool (``Out-of-fold Decision MLP
training features,'' Section~\ref{sec:eval}), so no stage of the pipeline
has ever seen the held-out family. Overall AUROC stays stable at
$0.925$--$0.952$ across all holdouts, but per-family detection

missed and \textsc{Allow}ed rather than escalated---the system is
confidently wrong on Ryuk, not appropriately uncertain. Honest

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Where the Framework Excels}

Strongest performance clusters around four properties, each quantified in
Sections~\ref{sec:results_agents}--\ref{sec:results_imbalance}: near-ceiling
in-distribution discrimination, both for the Semantic Agent on RDset and
for the Behavioural Agent across random, temporal, and hardware splits
(Table~\ref{tab:auroc_splits}), where the Decision MLP's value lies in the
uncertainty structure it exposes for triage rather than an AUROC gain
(Remark~\ref{rem:corpus_mean}); a fixed-threshold policy now usable on its
own thanks to the RansKnow-corrected Knowledge score, further improved by
Bayesian TPE (Table~\ref{tab:policy_compare}); low-FPR, near-ceiling
majority-vote session detection with negligible EWC forgetting
(Table~\ref{tab:ewc_feedback}, Section~\ref{sec:results_significance}); and
score-pool AUROC that stays $\geq 0.92$ even under severe induced class
imbalance (Section~\ref{sec:results_imbalance}).

\subsection{Where the Framework Struggles}\label{sec:limitations}

The framework's principal failure mode is distributional shift at the
family level (Section~\ref{sec:results_lofo}): since the Knowledge and
Semantic Agents' fixed corpus-mean scores (Remark~\ref{rem:corpus_mean})
carry no family-specific signal, the family-holdout AUROC drop is
consistent across every axis of the generalisation-profile radar
(Supplementary Section~S-VIII), not an outlier metric, and the framework
is not suitable for autonomous containment on entirely unseen families
without prior operator review. This risk is family-specific rather than
uniform (Darkside and LockBit transfer well; Ryuk and WannaCry do not)
and, critically, overturns previously-reported perfect zero-shot transfer
for Conti and Ryuk once evaluated with the family genuinely excluded from
Behavioural training.

A more fundamental limitation arises under concurrent execution
(Section~\ref{sec:eval}): neither agent can fully separate concurrent
traces without process-level attribution---a limitation shared with
Hirano and Kobayashi~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}. For the most adversarial
case (SDelete with REvil), this manifests as low Auto-Contain coverage
($16.7$--$24.0\%$, the lowest of any combination) rather than silent
misses: the triage policy escalates nearly all remaining windows, so no
window in the mix partition goes unaddressed, though the system still
cannot autonomously contain this scenario with confidence.

The uncertainty mechanism provides graceful degradation rather than
silent failure for most hard cases---escalation rises on unseen Conti
sub-variants (Section~\ref{sec:ood_variants}), concurrent-mix
combinations (Supplementary Section~S-VIII, where even the hardest case,
SDelete+REvil, escalates $83\%$ of its windows), and harder-to-transfer
LOFO families (Table~\ref{tab:lofo})---but this is not universal: Conti.F
and Ryuk under leave-one-family-out (worst detection rate of any held-out
family, yet only $1.7\%$ escalation) show the system can also be
confidently wrong rather than appropriately uncertain. The
uncertainty-driven safety net is thus a substantial reduction in
silent-failure risk, not a guarantee against it. Four additional,
secondary limitations---RanSMAP's dataset scope (no post-2022 families),
adversarial robustness against a triage-policy-aware attacker, the
Knowledge Agent's sample size, and calibration-set separation for
temperature scaling and TPE---are discussed in Supplementary
Section~S-XI.

\subsection{Comparison with Hirano and Kobayashi, Adaptive Thresholding, and Deployment}\label{sec:discussion_hirano}

The closest direct comparator, Hirano and Kobayashi's~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}
1D-CNN+LSTM (23D features, $30$-second window), reports F-score $0.856$ on
hardware-holdout versus our Behavioural CNN's AUROC $0.939$
(Table~\ref{tab:auroc_splits}) and mean recall $0.63$ versus their $0.56$
on the Conti sub-variant partition (indicative given threshold-dependent
vs.\ threshold-independent metrics); more importantly, on most sub-variants
where both frameworks struggle, ours escalates rather than misclassifies
via $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:ood_variants}), though this
does not hold uniformly (Conti.F, Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). An
extended comparison is in Supplementary Section~S-IV.

The Bayesian TPE policy sets thresholds $(\tau^*, \kappa^*)$ once on the
calibration split; online recalibration under concept drift
(Supplementary Section~S-V) is future work
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

Inference latency (Table~\ref{tab:latency}, Section~\ref{sec:runtime}) is
the honest cost of running the three agents \emph{sequentially} followed
by the Decision MLP, as our implementation does; parallelising the
components across separate devices could reduce wall-clock latency toward
the slowest single agent, but we report the measured sequential cost
rather than a projected parallel one. Per-sample complexity is therefore
$\mathcal{O}(T \cdot \sum_i P_i)$; offline training (CTGAN, TPE, EWC) is
incurred once at deployment (full derivations in Supplementary
Section~S-VII).

% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We have presented a heterogeneous multi-agent framework for adaptive
ransomware triage that treats uncertainty as a first-class control
variable. The four principal contributions---MC Dropout per-agent
uncertainty, Decision MLP fusion with CTGAN augmentation, Bayesian TPE
triage policy optimisation, and Peer Consultation with EWC continual
learning---collectively address the structural limitations of
single-modality binary detectors, achieving strong static-PE (AUROC
$0.996$) and runtime behavioural detection (AUROC $0.963$), and a $47\%$
reduction in unnecessary escalation over fixed-threshold baselines.
Because the Semantic and Knowledge Agents necessarily contribute a fixed,
label-independent corpus-mean score in the absence of real per-sample
pairing across RDset, RanSMAP, and RansKnow (Remark~\ref{rem:corpus_mean}),
fusion's demonstrated value in this evaluation setting is the
uncertainty-gated triage policy it supports, not incremental detection
AUROC or a calibration gain over the better-calibrated mean-fusion
baseline it replaces (ECE $0.017$ vs.\ $0.027$). When the system encounters novel families or
concurrent execution beyond its training distribution, epistemic
uncertainty $\sigma_{\max}$ rises and the triage policy routes to
\textsc{Escalate}---preserving analyst oversight where the model is least
reliable---while Mondrian conformal prediction provides a complementary
distribution-free statistical guarantee under exchangeability.

Future work will address process-specific telemetry to resolve the semantic
gap under concurrent execution, extension to post-2022 ransomware families
(Cl0p, BlackCat/ALPHV, LockBit 3.0), and online Bayesian recalibration of
triage thresholds under concept drift.
