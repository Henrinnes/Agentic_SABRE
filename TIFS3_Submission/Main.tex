\begin{abstract}
Ransomware detection under operational constraints demands more than a binary
verdict: it requires principled uncertainty estimation, heterogeneous evidence
fusion, and uncertainty-driven triage that balances autonomous containment
against analyst escalation. We present a three-agent framework addressing
these requirements through four coupled contributions. First, a Semantic CNN
on static PE embeddings, a Behavioural CNN on 16-dimensional hypervisor
telemetry, and a Knowledge CNN on ATT\&CK tactic embeddings each employ Monte
Carlo Dropout to produce per-sample predictive probabilities and epistemic
uncertainties. Second, a Decision MLP learns a calibrated aggregate risk score
from the six-dimensional score pool, reducing Expected Calibration Error from
$0.094$ to $0.018$; Conditional Tabular GAN (CTGAN) augmentation addresses
score-space imbalance without retraining base agents. Third, a four-threshold
triage policy optimised via Bayesian TPE reduces
the escalation rate from $85.3\%$ to $24.7\%$ while raising balanced accuracy from
$0.525$ to $0.866$. Fourth, a Peer Consultation mechanism mitigates knowledge
collapse under concurrent benign--ransomware execution, and an Expert Feedback
Loop with Elastic Weight Consolidation enables continual adaptation to novel
families with negligible catastrophic forgetting
($\Delta\text{AUPRC}{=}0.001$). Evaluated across four generalisation axes
over six independent seeds on RanSMAP, RDset, and RansKnow, the framework
achieves AUROC $0.996$ on static PE detection, AUROC $0.961$ on runtime
behavioural detection, and AUROC $0.980$ under temporal holdout.
Under family holdout, Decision MLP AUROC falls to $0.676$, revealing a
meaningful generalisation gap for unseen families; epistemic uncertainty
routes these novel-family samples to analyst escalation rather than
autonomous containment. A Mondrian conformal prediction wrapper provides
distribution-free coverage guarantees, auto-classifying $77\%$ of traffic
at $99\%$ coverage.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Ransomware detection, multi-agent systems, uncertainty quantification,
generative adversarial networks, continual learning, conformal prediction.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Ransomware attacks impose escalating operational and financial costs on
enterprises and critical infrastructure~\cite{beaman2021ransomware,cybersecurity_ventures_2022}.
State-of-the-art detectors---whether signature-based antivirus engines,
endpoint detection and response (EDR) platforms, or machine-learning
classifiers trained on file-level features---converge on a common
architectural limitation: they produce a single scalar confidence score and
map it to a binary verdict. When a novel variant or an obfuscated execution
pattern degrades that score silently, no signal informs the operator that the
verdict is unreliable.

We identify three structural gaps in the current state of practice:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{No principled uncertainty.} A softmax output from a
        deterministic neural network is a point estimate of class membership,
        not a probability over models. It cannot distinguish a confident
        correct prediction from a confident incorrect one.
  \item \textbf{Heuristic modality fusion.} Combining static PE features
        with runtime telemetry via equal-weight averaging discards the
        per-modality reliability signal; a semantic agent operating on
        encrypted PE sections and a behavioural agent observing obfuscated
        I/O patterns should not carry equal weight.
  \item \textbf{Binary triage.} An alert generated by every uncertain
        detection overwhelms analysts; equally, routing every uncertain
        detection to automatic containment risks business disruption.
\end{enumerate}

This paper presents a framework that addresses all three gaps through a
principled pipeline: per-agent uncertainty quantification via MC Dropout,
learned score fusion via a calibrated Decision MLP, and triage policy
optimisation via Bayesian TPE. The framework treats uncertainty as a
\emph{control variable} for deployment rather than a diagnostic afterthought.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{heterogeneous three-agent architecture} in which a Semantic
        CNN, a Behavioural CNN, and a Knowledge CNN each produce a predictive
        probability and an epistemic uncertainty via $T=10$ MC Dropout forward
        passes.
  \item A \textbf{Decision MLP fusion mechanism} with temperature calibration
        that replaces heuristic mean fusion, reducing ECE from $0.094$ to
        $0.018$, supported by CTGAN score-space augmentation to handle class
        imbalance at the fusion layer.
  \item A \textbf{formal triage policy} over $(\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max})$ space
        with four jointly optimised thresholds via Bayesian TPE, reducing
        escalation rate from $85.3\%$ to $24.7\%$ while improving balanced accuracy
        from $0.525$ to $0.866$.
  \item A \textbf{Peer Consultation} mechanism that prevents knowledge collapse
        under concurrent execution, and an \textbf{Expert Feedback Loop} with
        EWC enabling continual family adaptation without catastrophic
        forgetting.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Behavioural Ransomware Detection}

Hypervisor-level behavioural monitoring~\cite{HiranoK22,hirano2025ransmap}
captures low-level I/O and memory access patterns before encryption artefacts
surface in the filesystem, providing strong coverage of novel variants.
I/O Request Packet (IRP) monitors~\cite{scaife_cryptolock_2016} detect
read--encrypt--write sequences characteristic of bulk encryption but remain
vulnerable to slow-encrypt evasion that artificially suppresses throughput
statistics.
API-call-based classifiers~\cite{Fernando2024FeSAD,sgandurra_automated_2016}
construct fine-grained behavioural profiles from system-call sequences and
achieve strong accuracy on closed-world benchmarks, yet require
process-level instrumentation that incurs non-trivial overhead at scale.
Survey work~\cite{moussaileb2021survey,Ispahany2024Survey} confirms that
runtime behavioural methods are consistently more effective than static analysis
against obfuscated or packed ransomware, but notes that all existing systems
share a structural limitation: they produce a scalar confidence score
and provide no mechanism for expressing per-sample epistemic uncertainty
or routing uncertain detections to analyst escalation.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification in Detection}

Monte Carlo Dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout} re-activates dropout at test time,
treating each stochastic forward pass as a draw from an approximate posterior
over network weights; the variance of $T$ outputs approximates epistemic
uncertainty at the cost of $T\times$ the inference compute.
Gawlikowski et al.~\cite{gawlikowski2023Uncertainity} provide a systematic
taxonomy of neural-network uncertainty methods across four families---ensemble,
sampling, deterministic, and test-time augmentation---establishing that MC
Dropout and deep ensembles are the most broadly applicable.
Deep ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan_simple_2017} independently trained on
random initialisations achieve better marginal calibration than MC Dropout
but require retaining $M$ full model copies at inference.
For distribution-free guarantees, conformal prediction~\cite{papadopoulos_icp_2002}
and its class-conditional Mondrian variant~\cite{vovk_mondrian_2003} bound
coverage probability without parametric assumptions; Angelopoulos and
Bates~\cite{angelopoulos_conformal_risk_2022} generalise this to arbitrary
risk functions.
In the security domain, uncertainty estimation has been applied to network
anomaly detection~\cite{Doan2024uncertainBay} and intrusion classification,
but ransomware \emph{triage}---where the cost of an autonomous containment
error differs qualitatively from the cost of an escalation---has not previously
been framed as an uncertainty-conditioned routing problem.

\subsection{Multi-Agent and Ensemble Detection}

Ensemble methods for malware classification~\cite{sagi_ensemble_2018,
masum2022ransomware} aggregate multiple classifiers over a shared feature
space, improving robustness through majority vote or average-score fusion.
Multi-agent intrusion detection systems~\cite{MultiAgentAdaptiveDetection2023}
distribute the detection task across specialised agents that monitor different
network zones, combining alerts through a centralised coordinator.
Multi-modal ransomware detection combining static PE features, API-call
sequences, and file-system events has been explored~\cite{moussaileb2021survey},
but typically with equal-weight or attention-based fusion that does not
propagate per-modality reliability.

Our framework differs from all of the above in two structural respects.
First, the three agents operate on \emph{structurally heterogeneous} input
spaces ($\R^{384}$ PE embeddings, $\R^{16}$ runtime telemetry,
$\R^{23}$ ATT\&CK vectors) that cannot be concatenated without discarding
modality-specific structure.
Second, each agent contributes not only a probability but an \emph{epistemic
uncertainty}, which the Decision MLP uses implicitly to down-weight unreliable
modalities and which the orchestrator uses explicitly to route uncertain
cases to analyst escalation rather than autonomous containment.
Standard ensemble methods have no equivalent of this uncertainty-conditioned
routing.

\subsection{Generative Augmentation and Continual Learning}

Class imbalance in detection datasets is routinely addressed by
SMOTE~\cite{chawla2002smote} and Conditional Tabular GAN
(CTGAN)~\cite{xu_ctgan_2019}, which have been applied to fraud and
intrusion detection~\cite{fiore_using_gans_2019,lin_idsgan_2022}, consistently
outperforming SMOTE when the minority class is small and feature distributions
are non-Gaussian.
Our contribution applies CTGAN at the \emph{score space} output of the
multi-agent detector: operating on bounded $[0,1]^6$ probability--uncertainty
vectors constrains synthetic samples to the agents' output range, reducing
the risk of generating implausible score-uncertainty combinations and
eliminating raw telemetry reconstruction artefacts.

Catastrophic forgetting is the key barrier to continual adaptation: gradient
updates for a new task systematically overwrite prior weight configurations~\cite{kirkpatrick_ewc_2017}.
Temporal evaluation frameworks~\cite{pendlebury_tesseract_2019,jordaney_transcend_2017}
and systematic studies~\cite{Ceschin2022ConceptDrift} establish that all
standard classifiers lose substantial accuracy within months of the training
cutoff under concept drift.
Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick_ewc_2017} mitigates this
via a diagonal Fisher penalty that protects parameters important to retained tasks.
Our Expert Feedback Loop applies EWC to the Decision MLP, achieving
$\Delta\text{AUPRC}{=}0.001$ on retained families after five correction
iterations---enabling continual adaptation without retraining base agents.

\subsection{Relationship to Prior Work}

This paper extends the two-agent Agentic SABRE
framework~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa}, which combined a Semantic CNN and a
Behavioural CNN with MC Dropout uncertainty and a fixed triage policy.
The present work makes four technically distinct contributions absent from
the prior system: (1) a third Knowledge CNN agent on ATT\&CK tactic
embeddings, expanding the score pool from $[0,1]^4$ to $[0,1]^6$;
(2) Bayesian TPE optimisation of four jointly tuned triage thresholds
$(\tau_{\text{high}}, \tau, \kappa, \kappa_{\text{low}})$ replacing the
fixed $(\tau{=}0.10, \kappa{=}0.10)$ pair; (3) CTGAN score-space
augmentation at the fusion layer; and (4) EWC continual learning and
Mondrian conformal prediction wrappers.
All datasets, evaluation splits, experimental results, and figures are
independent of~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa}.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Detection Task}

Let $\mathcal{X} = \mathcal{X}_{\text{sem}} \times \mathcal{X}_{\text{beh}}
\times \mathcal{X}_{\text{know}}$ denote the joint input space of three
heterogeneous modalities, and let $\mathcal{Y} = \{0,1\}$ denote the binary
ransomware label. A sample is observed as a triple
$\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}_{\text{sem}}, \mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}},
\mathbf{x}_{\text{know}}) \in \mathcal{X}$, where

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf{x}_{\text{sem}} \in \R^{384}$ is a sentence-transformer
        embedding of Portable Executable (PE) section names;
  \item $\mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}} \in \R^{16}$ is a 16-dimensional feature
        vector extracted from a $T_{\text{window}}=10\,\text{s}$ telemetry
        window (see Section~\ref{sec:agents});
  \item $\mathbf{x}_{\text{know}} \in \R^{d}$ is an embedding of
        ATT\&CK tactic profiles derived from threat-intelligence transcripts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Triage Task}

Rather than mapping $\mathbf{x}$ to a binary label, the triage task maps
$\mathbf{x}$ to one of three actions $\mathcal{A} =
\{\textsc{Auto-Contain},\, \textsc{Escalate},\, \textsc{Allow}\}$ according
to a policy $\pi : (\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max}) \to \mathcal{A}$, where
$\hat{p} \in [0,1]$ is the fused risk score and $\sigma_{\max} \in [0,1]$ is
the maximum per-agent epistemic uncertainty.

\begin{definition}[Triage Policy]\label{def:policy}
Given thresholds $\Theta = (\tau_{\text{high}}, \tau, \kappa,
\kappa_{\text{low}})$ with $0 < \tau \leq \tau_{\text{high}} \leq 1$ and
$0 < \kappa_{\text{low}} \leq \kappa \leq 1$, the triage policy is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:policy}
  \pi(\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max}; \Theta) =
  \begin{cases}
    \textsc{Auto-Contain} & \hat{p} \geq \tau_{\text{high}}
                            \;\wedge\; \sigma_{\max} \leq \kappa_{\text{low}}, \\
    \textsc{Escalate}     & \hat{p} \geq \tau
                            \;\vee\; \sigma_{\max} > \kappa, \\
    \textsc{Allow}        & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Optimisation Objective}

Let $\text{bal\_acc}(\Theta)$ denote the balanced accuracy of the joint
detection--triage system under thresholds $\Theta$, and let
$\text{esc\_rate}(\Theta)$ denote the fraction of samples routed to
\textsc{Escalate}. The policy optimisation problem is:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
  \Theta^* = \argmax_{\Theta \in \mathcal{F}} \;
  J(\Theta) \coloneqq \text{bal\_acc}(\Theta)
  - \lambda\,\text{esc\_rate}(\Theta),
\end{equation}

where $\lambda \geq 0$ is an operational cost parameter encoding the relative
burden of analyst escalation, and $\mathcal{F}$ is the feasibility region
$\{\Theta : \tau \leq \tau_{\text{high}},\; \kappa_{\text{low}} \leq
\kappa\}$.

\begin{remark}
The parameter $\lambda$ is a deployment decision, not a modelling one. An
organisation with constrained analyst capacity sets higher $\lambda$; one
prioritising missed-detection avoidance sets $\lambda \approx 0$.
\end{remark}

\section{Agent Architecture and Uncertainty Quantification}\label{sec:agents}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Monte Carlo Dropout as Approximate Bayesian Inference}

Let $f_\theta : \mathcal{X}_i \to [0,1]$ denote a neural network with
parameters $\theta$ trained on modality $i$. At inference, standard dropout
is disabled, yielding a deterministic point estimate. MC Dropout
\cite{gal2016dropout} instead keeps dropout \emph{active} at test time,
treating each stochastic forward pass as a draw from an approximate posterior
$q_\phi(\theta) \approx p(\theta \mid \mathcal{D})$ over network weights.

Formally, for $T$ stochastic forward passes with dropout masks
$\{\mathbf{z}_t\}_{t=1}^T$:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:mc_mean}
  \hat{p}_i(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T}
    f_{\theta \odot \mathbf{z}_t}(\mathbf{x}),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:mc_std}
  \sigma_i(\mathbf{x}) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T-1} \sum_{t=1}^{T}
    \left(f_{\theta \odot \mathbf{z}_t}(\mathbf{x}) - \hat{p}_i(\mathbf{x})
    \right)^2},
\end{equation}

where $\odot$ denotes element-wise masking. The quantity $\sigma_i$ captures
\emph{epistemic} uncertainty---reducible uncertainty arising from limited
training data---as distinct from \emph{aleatoric} uncertainty inherent to the
data generating process~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties}.

\begin{proposition}[Epistemic Signal]\label{prop:epistemic}
When a test sample $\mathbf{x}$ lies far from the training manifold of agent
$i$ (i.e., a novel ransomware family), we hypothesise that the variance
$\sigma_i^2(\mathbf{x})$ tends to increase with distributional shift
$d(\mathbf{x}, \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}})$~\cite{gal2016dropout}.
Approximate Bayesian methods do not provide a formal monotonicity guarantee
in general; we verify this tendency empirically across family and hardware
holdout splits (Supplementary Section~S-VI).
\end{proposition}

We use $T=10$ passes per agent, empirically verified to stabilise $\sigma_i$
to within $1\%$ of the $T=50$ estimate (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
Supplementary Section~S-VI quantifies this
stability and the associated latency trade-off.


\subsection{Semantic Agent}

% ── Architecture ──
The Semantic Agent operates on static PE structure. Given a PE file, section
names are concatenated and encoded via a frozen sentence transformer
(all-MiniLM-L6-v2) to produce $\mathbf{x}_{\text{sem}} \in \R^{384}$.
A Semantic CNN $f_{\text{sem}}$ with MC Dropout maps this embedding to
$(\hat{p}_{\text{sem}}, \sigma_{\text{sem}})$.

% ── Training data ──
Training data: RDset~\cite{kabuye2025explainable,mathur2020ransomware}, comprising $138{,}047$ PE files
($41{,}123$ ransomware; $96{,}924$ benign) with SMOTE balancing applied at
the feature level prior to training.

\subsection{Behavioural Agent}

% ── Feature vector ──
The Behavioural Agent operates on runtime storage and memory access patterns
collected via a thin BitVisor hypervisor~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}. Within
each $T_{\text{window}}=10\,\text{s}$ window we compute a
$\mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}} \in \R^{16}$ feature vector partitioned as
$\mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}} = (\chi_s, \chi_m)$, where:

\textbf{Storage features} $\chi_s \in \R^{5}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:storage_features}
  \chi_s = \Bigl(
    \bar{T}_{s,r},\;
    \bar{T}_{s,w},\;
    V_{s,r},\;
    V_{s,w},\;
    \bar{H}_{s,w}
  \Bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{T}_{s,r}$ and $\bar{T}_{s,w}$ are mean read/write throughputs
$[\text{byte/s}]$; $V_{s,r}$ and $V_{s,w}$ are variances of accessed Logical
Block Addresses (LBA); and $\bar{H}_{s,w}$ is mean Shannon entropy of written
sectors, computed as $H(X) = -\sum_i p(x_i) \log_2 p(x_i)$.

\textbf{Memory features} $\chi_m \in \R^{11}$:
\begin{align}\label{eq:memory_features}
  \chi_m = \Bigl(
    &H_{m,w},\; H_{m,rw},\;
     C_{4K,r},\; C_{4K,w},\; C_{4K,rw},\; C_{4K,x},\nonumber\\
    &C_{2M,r},\; C_{2M,rw},\;
     C_{\text{MMIO},r},\; C_{\text{MMIO},w},\;
     V_{m,w}
  \Bigr),
\end{align}
where $H_{m,w}$ and $H_{m,rw}$ are Shannon entropies of written and
read/written memory pages; $C_{s,o}$ denotes the count of memory accesses of
size $s \in \{4\,\text{KiB},\,2\,\text{MiB},\,\text{MMIO}\}$ and operation
$o \in \{r,w,rw,x\}$; and $V_{m,w}$ is the variance of written Guest Physical
Addresses (GPA).

% ── Training data ──
Training data: RanSMAP~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}, comprising $35{,}857$
ten-second windows from $1{,}776$ sessions across 15 hardware configurations,
six ransomware families (Conti, Darkside, LockBit, REvil, Ryuk, WannaCry),
and six benign applications (AESCrypt, Firefox, Idle, Office, SDelete, Zip).

\subsection{Knowledge Agent}

% ── Architecture ──
The Knowledge Agent encodes ATT\&CK tactic profiles derived from
threat-intelligence transcripts (RansKnow~\cite{ransknow_2026}: $307$
ATT\&CK-aligned transcripts). A Discourse CNN $f_{\text{know}}$ maps the
tactic embedding $\mathbf{x}_{\text{know}} \in \R^{d}$ to
$(\hat{p}_{\text{know}}, \sigma_{\text{know}})$.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Generalisation Splits}

We define four disjoint evaluation protocols on the RanSMAP session space
$\mathcal{S}$:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Random:} 80/20 stratified split over $\mathcal{S}$.
  \item \textbf{Family holdout:} $\mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} =
        \{s \in \mathcal{S} : \text{family}(s) \in
        \{\text{REvil},\text{WannaCry},\text{Firefox},\text{Zip}\}\}$.
  \item \textbf{Hardware holdout:} $\mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} =
        \{s \in \mathcal{S} : \text{hw}(s) \in
        \{\text{i5-gen12},\text{i7-gen12},\text{i3-gen12/DDR5}\}\}$.
  \item \textbf{Temporal:} $\mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} =
        \{s \in \mathcal{S} : t(s) \geq t_{0.85}\}$,
        where $t_{0.85}$ is the 85th percentile timestamp.
\end{enumerate}

All results are reported as mean $\pm$ std over $K=6$ independent random
seeds.

\section{Decision MLP Fusion and Score-Space Augmentation}\label{sec:fusion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Score Pool Construction}

After MC Dropout inference by all three agents, we form the score pool vector:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score_pool}
  \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}) = \bigl(
    \hat{p}_{\text{sem}},\; \sigma_{\text{sem}},\;
    \hat{p}_{\text{beh}},\; \sigma_{\text{beh}},\;
    \hat{p}_{\text{know}},\; \sigma_{\text{know}}
  \bigr) \in [0,1]^6.
\end{equation}
The maximum per-agent uncertainty $\sigma_{\max} =
\max(\sigma_{\text{sem}},\sigma_{\text{beh}},\sigma_{\text{know}})$
is derived from $\mathbf{v}$ and used independently in the triage
policy (Section~\ref{sec:triage}), but is not fed to the Decision MLP.

\begin{remark}[Why Score Space]
Fusing in score space---rather than feature space---preserves the
per-modality uncertainty structure. Feature-space concatenation of
$\R^{384} \oplus \R^{16} \oplus \R^d$ would conflate structurally
incompatible representations and preclude independent uncertainty propagation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{CTGAN Score-Space Augmentation}

Let $\mathcal{V}_{\text{train}} = \{(\mathbf{v}_j, y_j)\}_{j=1}^{N}$ denote
the score-pool training set with labels $y_j \in \{0,1\}$, where the
ransomware class is a fraction $r \ll 0.5$ of samples. We apply Conditional
Tabular GAN (CTGAN)~\cite{xu_ctgan_2019} to generate synthetic minority-class
score-pool vectors.

The CTGAN minimax objective is:
\begin{align}\label{eq:ctgan}
  \min_G \max_D \;
  &\E_{\mathbf{v} \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(\mathbf{v})] \nonumber\\
  +\; &\E_{\mathbf{z} \sim p_z,\, c \sim p_c}[\log(1-D(G(\mathbf{z},c)))]
  - \gamma \Vert \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{v}}} D(\hat{\mathbf{v}}) \Vert^2,
\end{align}
where $c$ is a conditional class label, $\mathbf{z}$ is a latent noise vector,
$\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ is a linear interpolation between real and generated
samples, and $\gamma$ is the gradient-penalty coefficient.

Crucially, CTGAN operates on $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^6$, not on raw features:
this ensures that the generator cannot produce physically impossible score
combinations and preserves the calibration structure of the base agents.

\subsection{Decision MLP Architecture}

The Decision MLP $g_\phi : [0,1]^6 \to [0,1]$ maps the score pool to a
fused risk score:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mlp}
  g_\phi(\mathbf{v}) = \sigma\!\left(
    \mathbf{W}_2 \,\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{b}_1)
    + \mathbf{b}_2
  \right),
\end{equation}
with dimensions $6 \to 32 \to 1$ and sigmoid output. Input $\mathbf{v}$
is standardised to zero mean and unit variance per component before
training; the scaler statistics are stored alongside the model artifact
for inference.

\subsection{Temperature Calibration}

A deterministic neural classifier is not guaranteed to be calibrated even
after training. We apply temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration}: the
logit $\ell = \log(\hat{p}/(1-\hat{p}))$ is divided by a learned scalar
$T > 0$ before the final sigmoid:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:temperature}
  \hat{p}_{\text{cal}} = \sigma(\ell / T),
  \quad T^* = \argmin_T \; \mathrm{NLL}_T(\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}).
\end{equation}

Expected Calibration Error is defined as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ece}
  \ECE = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \frac{|B_m|}{N}
    \left| \text{acc}(B_m) - \text{conf}(B_m) \right|,
\end{equation}
where $\{B_m\}_{m=1}^M$ partition $[0,1]$ into $M$ equal-width confidence
bins, $\text{acc}(B_m)$ is empirical accuracy and $\text{conf}(B_m)$ is mean
predicted confidence within bin $B_m$.

After calibration, ECE decreases from $0.094$ (mean fusion baseline) to
$0.018$ (Decision MLP with temperature scaling).

\section{Triage Policy and Bayesian Optimisation}\label{sec:triage}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Triage Policy in $(\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max})$ Space}

The triage policy $\pi$ (Definition~\ref{def:policy}) partitions the unit
square $[0,1]^2$ into three regions. The fused risk $\hat{p} =
g_\phi(\mathbf{v})$ and maximum agent uncertainty $\sigma_{\max}$ jointly
determine the action. The boundary conditions ensure that:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{Auto-Contain} requires \emph{both} high risk \emph{and} low
        uncertainty---autonomous action is taken only when the system is
        jointly confident in detection and reliable;
  \item \textsc{Escalate} fires when \emph{either} the risk score is elevated
        ($\hat{p} \geq \tau$) \emph{or} agent disagreement is high
        ($\sigma_{\max} > \kappa$), routing borderline and uncertain samples to
        an analyst rather than acting autonomously;
  \item \textsc{Allow} covers the confident-benign region.
\end{itemize}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triage_space} illustrates the three regions geometrically and
shows the distribution of training-distribution ransomware, benign, and OOD
samples in $(\hat{p}, \sigma_{\max})$ space.

\subsection{Bayesian Triage Policy Optimisation via TPE}

The objective $J(\Theta)$ in~\eqref{eq:objective} is non-differentiable with
respect to $\Theta$ (it involves discrete triage counts). We therefore employ
Bayesian optimisation via Tree-structured Parzen Estimation
(TPE)~\cite{bergstra_algorithms_2011}.

TPE models the probability density of $J$ conditioned on $\Theta$ as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tpe}
  p(\Theta \mid J) =
  \begin{cases}
    \ell(\Theta) & J(\Theta) < J^*, \\
    g(\Theta)    & J(\Theta) \geq J^*,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $J^*$ is the $\gamma$-quantile of observed objective values, $\ell$ is
a Parzen-window density over good configurations, and $g$ is a density over
poor ones. The acquisition criterion selects:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ei}
  \Theta_{\text{next}} = \argmax_\Theta
  \frac{\ell(\Theta)}{g(\Theta)},
\end{equation}
which approximates the Expected Improvement criterion.

We run $300$ TPE trials over $\mathcal{F}$ with $\lambda = 0.3$; the search
budget is chosen based on the dimensionality of $\Theta$ and the cost of
evaluating $J$ on the validation set (approximately $0.15\,\text{s}$ per
trial). Results and convergence analysis are presented in
Section~\ref{sec:results_tpe}.

\section{Peer Consultation and Expert Feedback Loop}\label{sec:feedback}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Peer Consultation}

Under concurrent benign--ransomware execution, the Behavioural Agent observes
superimposed access patterns, inflating $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ and
suppressing $\hat{p}_{\text{beh}}$. To prevent \emph{knowledge collapse}
(a single-agent failure cascading to an incorrect triage decision), we
introduce Peer Consultation (PC).

\begin{definition}[Peer Consultation]\label{def:pc}
When agent $i$'s epistemic uncertainty $\sigma_i(\mathbf{x}) > \kappa_{\text{PC}}$
for a threshold $\kappa_{\text{PC}} > 0$, agent $i$'s score is replaced by a
weighted combination of its peer agents' scores:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peer_consultation}
  \hat{p}_{i}^{\text{PC}}(\mathbf{x}) =
    \sum_{j \neq i} w_j\,\hat{p}_{j}(\mathbf{x}),
\end{equation}
where weights are proportional to the inverse uncertainty of each peer,
$w_j \propto 1/\sigma_j$, normalised so that $\sum_{j \neq i} w_j = 1$.
Each agent maintains its own threshold $\kappa_{\text{PC}}^{(i)}$; in
practice all are set equal to the triage threshold $\kappa$ optimised by
Bayesian TPE (Section~\ref{sec:triage}).
\end{definition}

Peer Consultation does not suppress the triggering agent's uncertainty;
rather, it routes the sample to the \textsc{Escalate} action while ensuring
the score pool reflects available evidence from the more reliable modalities.

\subsection{Expert Feedback Loop with EWC}

The Expert Feedback Loop allows an analyst-approved correction label
$\tilde{y}$ for an escalated sample to update the Decision MLP $g_\phi$
incrementally, without catastrophic forgetting of the base agents' knowledge.

We apply Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick_ewc_2017}.
Let $\phi^*$ be the parameters of $g_\phi$ trained on the original dataset
$\mathcal{D}_0$, and let $F_j$ denote the diagonal Fisher information for
parameter $j$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fisher}
  F_j = \E_{\mathbf{v} \sim \mathcal{D}_0}\!\left[
    \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial \phi_j}
    \log p(y \mid \mathbf{v}; \phi^*)\right)^2
  \right].
\end{equation}

The EWC loss for the incremental update on correction set
$\mathcal{D}_{\text{new}}$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ewc}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{EWC}}(\phi) =
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\phi; \mathcal{D}_{\text{new}})
    + \frac{\lambda_{\text{EWC}}}{2}
      \sum_{j} F_j \left(\phi_j - \phi^*_j\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$ is the cross-entropy loss on new samples and
$\lambda_{\text{EWC}}$ controls the trade-off between plasticity and
stability. We measure catastrophic forgetting as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forgetting}
  \Delta\text{AUPRC} =
    \text{AUPRC}(\phi; \mathcal{D}_0) -
    \text{AUPRC}(\phi_{\text{new}}; \mathcal{D}_0),
\end{equation}
achieving $\Delta\text{AUPRC} = 0.001$ on retained families after five
feedback iterations.

\section{Conformal Prediction Wrapper}\label{sec:conformal}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Mondrian Conformal Prediction}

To provide distribution-free coverage guarantees on triage outcomes, we wrap
the fused output $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}}$ in a Mondrian conformal predictor.
Let $\alpha \in (0,1)$ be the target miscoverage rate.

\begin{definition}[Conformal Prediction Set]\label{def:cp}
Given a calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(\mathbf{v}_i,
y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$, define non-conformity scores $s_i =
1 - f_y(\mathbf{v}_i)$ for the true class $y_i$. The conformal prediction
set at level $\alpha$ for a new sample $\mathbf{v}$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp_set}
  \mathcal{C}_\alpha(\mathbf{v}) = \bigl\{y \in \mathcal{Y} :
    s(\mathbf{v}, y) \leq Q_{1-\alpha}(\{s_i\}_{i=1}^n)\bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $Q_{1-\alpha}$ is the $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of calibration scores.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Marginal Coverage Guarantee]\label{thm:coverage}
Under exchangeability of calibration and test samples:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coverage}
  \Prob\!\left(Y \in \mathcal{C}_\alpha(\mathbf{v})\right) \geq 1 - \alpha.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Mondrian conformal prediction stratifies the calibration non-conformity scores
by predicted class, conditioning~\eqref{eq:cp_set} separately for each label.
Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage} provides a formal \emph{marginal} coverage guarantee
under exchangeability of calibration and test samples; Mondrian stratification
yields class-conditional coverage empirically but does not carry a separate
distribution-free guarantee beyond the marginal bound.
The exchangeability assumption holds for the random-stratified split; under
temporal and family holdout, the formal guarantee does not apply and coverage
is verified empirically.
At $\alpha = 0.01$ ($99\%$ coverage), $77\%$ of traffic is auto-classified with
$99.1\%$ detection precision, and the remaining $23\%$ is escalated for analyst review.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Datasets}\label{sec:datasets}

\textbf{RDset}~\cite{kabuye2025explainable,mathur2020ransomware}: $138{,}047$ Windows Portable Executable (PE)
files ($41{,}123$ ransomware; $96{,}924$ benign). Used exclusively for
Semantic Agent training and evaluation. Disjoint train/test splits at the
binary (file-hash) level.

\textbf{RanSMAP}~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}: $35{,}857$ ten-second execution
windows from $1{,}776$ sessions across 15 hardware configurations (four CPUs:
Celeron G3920, Core i3-12100, Core i5-12500, Core i7-12700; six DDR4/DDR5
frequencies; two RAM capacities). Six ransomware families (Conti, Darkside,
LockBit, REvil, Ryuk, WannaCry) and six benign applications (AESCrypt,
Firefox, Idle, Office, SDelete, Zip) collected under BitVisor hypervisor
surveillance. A separate \texttt{variants} partition provides $1{,}397$
windows from seven Conti sub-variants for out-of-distribution (OOD)
generalisation analysis; a \texttt{mix} partition provides $600$ windows from
ten concurrent execution combinations. Neither partition is used for training.

\textbf{RansKnow}~\cite{ransknow_2026}: $307$ ATT\&CK-aligned
threat-intelligence transcripts. Used exclusively for Knowledge Agent
training.

\textbf{Dataset characteristics and imbalance.} RanSMAP exhibits moderate
class imbalance; SMOTE balancing is applied at the feature level prior to
Behavioural Agent training. RDset exhibits $\approx\!30\%$ ransomware
prevalence. Score-space imbalance at the fusion layer is addressed by CTGAN
augmentation (Section~\ref{sec:fusion}).

\subsection{Implementation Details}

\textbf{Semantic CNN.}
Input: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{sem}} \in \R^{384}$, treated as a single-channel
sequence of length 384.
Architecture: Conv1d$(384{\to}256,\,k{=}3)$--ReLU--Dropout$(p{=}0.3)$;
Conv1d$(256{\to}128,\,k{=}3)$--ReLU--Dropout$(p{=}0.3)$;
Conv1d$(128{\to}64,\,k{=}3)$--ReLU--Dropout$(p{=}0.3)$;
GlobalAvgPool; FC$(64{\to}1)$; Sigmoid.
SMOTE applied to RDset at the embedding level ($r_{\text{minority}}=0.30$).

\textbf{Behavioural CNN.}
Input: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{beh}} \in \R^{16}$, treated as a single-channel
sequence of length 16.
Architecture: three convolutional blocks
(Conv1d--BatchNorm--ReLU--MaxPool at stages 1--2; Dropout$(p{=}0.3)$ at
stage 3); GlobalAvgPool; FC$(128{\to}1)$; Sigmoid.
Total parameters: $8{,}385$ ($32.8\,\text{KB}$).
SMOTE applied to RanSMAP training windows.

\textbf{Knowledge CNN.}
Input: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{know}} \in \R^{23}$, the 23-dimensional ATT\&CK
tactic profile ($d{=}23$).
Architecture mirrors the Behavioural CNN, scaled to 23-dim input.
Trained on 307 RansKnow transcripts (70/15/15 train/val/test by family label).

\textbf{Decision MLP.}
Input: $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^6$ (standardised before each forward pass).
Architecture: FC$(6{\to}32)$--ReLU--Dropout$(p{=}0.2)$; FC$(32{\to}1)$--Sigmoid.
Temperature scaling applied post-hoc to the raw logit via L-BFGS on
validation set.

\textbf{Training protocol.}
All networks: Adam optimiser, $\eta = 10^{-3}$, batch size $64$,
maximum $50$ epochs with early stopping (patience $= 6$ epochs on
held-out validation loss); best weights restored before evaluation.
$K = 6$ independent random seeds for all RanSMAP experiments.

\textbf{CTGAN.}
Fitted independently on ransomware and benign subsets of the score-pool
training split; $50$ epochs per class; $N_{\text{synth}} = 2{,}000$
(1,000 per class); synthetic rows clipped to $[0,1]^6$ and NaN rows removed.
Implemented with \texttt{ctgan} v0.9 and Optuna 3.5.

\textbf{Bayesian TPE.}
$300$ Optuna trials; TPE sampler; MedianPruner with 15-trial warm-up;
$\lambda = 0.3$.

\textbf{EWC.}
$\lambda_{\text{EWC}} = 300$; diagonal Fisher estimated on $1{,}000$
random samples from $\mathcal{D}_0$.

\textbf{Hardware.}
NVIDIA GB10 GPU, Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.2.
Inference latency: $500$ repetitions, $50$ warm-up passes, batch size $32$.

\subsection{Statistical Significance of Fusion Gain}\label{sec:results_significance}

Table~\ref{tab:generalisation} reports mean AUROC but does not address whether
the Decision MLP's improvement over the Behavioural CNN is statistically reliable.
We assess this with two complementary tests.
\textbf{(1) One-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test:} for each split we treat the six
per-seed Behavioural CNN AUROCs as samples and test whether their median is
significantly below the Decision MLP reference AUROC (H$_1$: MLP $>$ B).
\textbf{(2) McNemar's test} (continuity-corrected $\chi^2$) on per-window binary
predictions (threshold $0.5$) for the random-stratified split.

Results are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ea_significance}.
On random-stratified, hardware-holdout, and temporal-holdout, the Wilcoxon test
rejects the null at $p{=}0.016$: the Decision MLP ($0.976$, $0.962$, $0.980$)
consistently outperforms the Behavioural CNN alone
($0.961{\pm}0.003$, $0.939{\pm}0.006$, $0.960{\pm}0.005$) across all seeds.
On family holdout, the result \emph{reverses}: the Behavioural CNN
($0.707{\pm}0.010$) outperforms the Decision MLP ($0.676{\pm}0.001$, $p{=}1.00$).
This confirms that fusion with the Knowledge Agent's corpus-mean fallback for
unseen families \emph{degrades} rather than improves discrimination---a finding
with direct operational implications: autonomous fusion should be gated on
family recognition confidence.
McNemar's test on random-stratified ($b{=}200$, $c{=}299$, $\chi^2{=}19.2$,
$p{<}10^{-4}$) confirms that the MLP corrects significantly more window-level
errors than it introduces at the per-sample level.

\subsection{Risk-Coverage Analysis}\label{sec:results_aurc}

AUROC summarises discrimination across all thresholds, but in selective
prediction the operator cares specifically about \emph{precision at a given
coverage level}.
We compute the Area Under the Risk-Coverage curve (AURC), sweeping the
auto-contain threshold $\tau$ from $1.0$ to $0.0$: coverage is the fraction
of windows assigned $\hat{p}_{\text{MLP}} \geq \tau$, and precision is the
ransomware fraction among those auto-contained windows.

Results are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:eb_risk_coverage}.
On random-stratified the Decision MLP achieves AURC $0.843$; hardware-holdout
is nearly identical ($0.841$), confirming stable precision-at-coverage across
recording platforms.
Temporal holdout ($0.693$) and family holdout ($0.644$) are lower, reflecting
the moderate and severe distribution shifts respectively.
Notably, at zero coverage (all windows escalated) precision is
$100\%$ by construction; the sharp precision drop as $\tau$ falls below $0.9$
on family holdout signals that the MLP's confidence is less well-calibrated for
novel families, consistent with the calibration findings of Section~\ref{sec:results_fusion}.

\subsection{Session-Level Detection and Latency}\label{sec:results_session}

Window-level metrics measure discrimination, but the operator's primary concern
is per-session detection and time-to-first-alert.
We aggregate Decision MLP predictions to the session level (a contiguous
execution recording) using two rules.
\textbf{Max-score:} a session is flagged if any window's
$\hat{p}_{\text{MLP}} \geq 0.5$.
\textbf{Majority-vote:} a session is flagged if $>50\%$ of its windows
satisfy $\hat{p}_{\text{MLP}} \geq 0.5$.
Latency to first detection is defined as the index of the first flagged window
multiplied by the $10\,\text{s}$ window stride.

Results are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ed_session_level}.
On in-distribution splits, \textbf{majority-vote} achieves near-perfect
session-level detection (random: $98.5\%$, hardware: $91.1\%$, temporal:
$98.9\%$) at session FPR $0.0$ -- no benign session is misclassified.
\textbf{Max-score} achieves $100\%$ detection across all in-distribution splits
but at elevated FPR ($0.58$--$0.60$): a single high-probability window in any
benign session suffices to trigger an alert.
Majority-vote is therefore the preferred operating mode in practice.

On family holdout, majority-vote detection falls to $45.9\%$ with FPR $3.8\%$,
consistent with the window-level AUROC degradation on unseen families.
Latency to first detection has \textbf{median $0\,\text{s}$} on all
in-distribution splits (ransomware is identified in the very first $10\,\text{s}$
window for the majority of sessions), rising to median $10\,\text{s}$ on
family holdout where additional observational windows are needed.
Full session-level balanced accuracy across all splits is provided in
Fig.~\ref{fig:ed_session_level}.

\subsection{Simultaneous Execution}

The \texttt{mix} partition of RanSMAP provides $600$ windows from ten
concurrent-execution combinations (ransomware + benign application running
simultaneously on the same host), constituting the hardest generalisation
challenge in the benchmark.

Under concurrent execution, the Behavioural Agent observes superimposed
I/O and memory access patterns from both processes, inflating
$\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ and suppressing $\hat{p}_{\text{beh}}$.
The most adversarial scenario---AESCrypt+REvil---produces a detection
rate of only $1.5\%$ without Peer Consultation: AESCrypt's legitimate
bulk encryption overwhelms REvil's signal, and the Knowledge Agent's
near-zero probability for REvil (absent from RansKnow) further degrades
the mean-fusion score below $\tau$.

With Peer Consultation active, the Knowledge Agent's probability is
replaced by the inverse-uncertainty-weighted average of $\hat{p}_{\text{sem}}$
and $\hat{p}_{\text{beh}}$, recovering the Semantic Agent's
partial detection signal.
Detection rate improves to $4.2\%$ for AESCrypt+REvil, while
SDelete+REvil rises from $27.1\%$ to $31.8\%$.
Although absolute concurrent-execution detection rates remain low,
the escalation rate rises to $>60\%$ for all concurrent-execution scenarios
under Bayesian TPE thresholds, ensuring that the system routes these
uncertain cases to analyst review rather than autonomous containment.
This compares favourably with Hirano and Kobayashi~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}, whose
detector produces a binary output and has no mechanism to express the
ambiguity introduced by concurrent benign processes.

\paragraph{Coverage under distribution shift.}
The formal marginal coverage guarantee of Mondrian conformal prediction holds
under the exchangeability assumption between calibration and test samples.
Table~\ref{tab:conformal_shift} reports empirical coverage and escalation rate
across all four splits, with thresholds calibrated on the split-specific
validation set and evaluated on the corresponding test set.

On in-distribution splits, empirical coverage at $\alpha{=}0.01$ is
$99.1\%$, $98.4\%$, and $99.3\%$ for random-stratified, hardware-holdout,
and temporal-holdout respectively---within $0.7$ percentage points of the
$99\%$ nominal target, consistent with the marginal guarantee under
approximate exchangeability.

Family holdout is the critical exception: empirical coverage at $\alpha{=}0.01$
collapses to $\mathbf{75.0\%}$, a shortfall of $24$ percentage points from the
nominal target, and falls further to $49.8\%$ and $35.4\%$ at $\alpha{=}0.05$
and $\alpha{=}0.10$ respectively.
This confirms that the exchangeability assumption is violated when test families
are entirely absent from the calibration set---the conformal thresholds derived
from known families cannot correctly account for the shifted score distribution
of novel families.
These results extend the observation in~\cite{angelopoulos2021uncertainty} that
conformal guarantees degrade under covariate shift, and demonstrate empirically
that family-level distributional shift constitutes a particularly severe violation
in the ransomware domain.
\emph{We therefore recommend treating Mondrian conformal coverage as an
in-distribution validity certificate rather than a universal bound;} for unseen
families, the escalation mechanism (via $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ thresholding)
provides a complementary, uncertainty-driven safety net.

\subsection{Ablation Study}\label{sec:ablation}

Four ablation studies validate key design choices.
\textbf{(1) MC Dropout pass count $T$:} at $T{=}10$, $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$
noise falls to $0.9\%$ of its mean---within $1\%$ of the $T{=}50$ estimate
and adding only $1.42\,\text{ms}$ overhead over a deterministic pass.
$T{=}1$ degenerates the triage policy to two actions (no uncertainty signal).
\textbf{(2) Feature ablation:} storage-only ($\chi_s$, 5D) achieves AUROC
$0.851$; memory-only ($\chi_m$, 11D) achieves $0.903$; the combined 16D
vector achieves $\mathbf{0.961}$, confirming complementarity.
\textbf{(3) Decision MLP depth:} one hidden layer ($6{\to}32{\to}1$, ECE
$0.021$) versus two ($6{\to}32{\to}16{\to}1$, ECE $0.018$) versus three
($6{\to}32{\to}16{\to}8{\to}1$, ECE $0.019$) are statistically equivalent
on AUROC; the one-layer variant is used in all experiments to match the
prior two-agent architecture~\cite{kabuye2025sabre_eswa}.
\textbf{(4) CTGAN ratio:} varying $N_{\text{synth}} \in [500,5000]$ produces
no statistically significant change in AUROC or ECE; we adopt
$N_{\text{synth}}{=}2{,}000$ as a conservative default.
Full ablation figures and tables are provided in Supplementary Section~S-VI.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Where the Framework Excels}

Strongest performance occurs in four areas.
First, the Semantic Agent achieves AUROC $0.996$ on RDset, confirming that
sentence-transformer PE embeddings are highly effective discriminators within
the training family distribution (AUROC $0.996$ on RDset).
Second, the Decision MLP generalises within distribution: temporal-holdout
AUROC $0.980$ and hardware-holdout AUROC $0.962$ match or exceed
random-stratified performance (Table~\ref{tab:generalisation}), with the
fusion gain over the Behavioural CNN alone confirmed as statistically significant
($p{=}0.016$, one-sample Wilcoxon, Section~\ref{sec:results_significance}).
Third, the Decision MLP reduces calibration error $5.2\times$ over mean
fusion ($\text{ECE}=0.018$ vs.\ $0.094$), enabling $35.3\%$ autonomous
classification at the TPE-optimised operating point versus $0\%$ under
fixed-threshold baselines, with risk-coverage AURC $0.843$ on the
random-stratified split (Section~\ref{sec:results_aurc}).
Fourth, at the session level, majority-vote aggregation achieves zero session
FPR with $\geq 91\%$ detection rate across all in-distribution splits, and
median latency to first detection is $0\,\text{s}$---ransomware is identified
in the very first $10\,\text{s}$ window for most sessions
(Section~\ref{sec:results_session}).
EWC limits catastrophic forgetting to $\Delta\text{AUPRC}=0.001$ across
five feedback iterations (Table~\ref{tab:ewc_feedback}), permitting
incremental operational adaptation.

\subsection{Where the Framework Struggles}

The framework's principal failure mode is distributional shift at the family
level.
Under family holdout, the Behavioural CNN drops to AUROC $0.707$ and the
Decision MLP to $0.676$---degradations of $0.254$ and $0.300$ from
random-stratified performance respectively.
Fusion does not recover this gap: the Knowledge Agent's corpus-mean fallback
for unseen families injects noise into the score pool, and the Decision MLP's
learned weighting cannot fully compensate.
The framework is therefore not suitable for autonomous containment decisions
on ransomware families entirely absent from training without prior operator
review.

Concurrent execution presents a more fundamental limitation.
When ransomware and a benign application run simultaneously on the same host,
the hypervisor-level telemetry aggregates both processes' I/O and memory
events.
The Behavioural Agent observes superimposed signals, suppressing
$\hat{p}_{\text{beh}}$ and inflating $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$.
For the most adversarial case---AESCrypt running concurrently with
REvil---detection rate reaches only $1.5\%$ without Peer Consultation and
$4.2\%$ with it (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
This limitation is shared with Hirano and Kobayashi~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}:
hypervisor-level telemetry without process-level attribution cannot
cleanly separate concurrent execution traces.

The session-level analysis (Section~\ref{sec:results_session}) and
risk-coverage curves (Section~\ref{sec:results_aurc}) quantify this degradation
in deployment-relevant terms.
On family holdout, majority-vote session detection falls to $45.9\%$ (vs.\
$98.5\%$ on random-stratified) and AURC drops to $0.644$ (vs.\ $0.843$),
meaning that when the model \emph{does} auto-contain, precision is lower and
coverage requires a much higher confidence threshold.
The conformal coverage analysis (Section~\ref{sec:results_conformal}) further
confirms that the formal exchangeability guarantee is violated under family shift:
empirical coverage collapses from $99.1\%$ to $75.0\%$ at $\alpha{=}0.01$,
so the conformal wrapper's safety certificate no longer holds for novel families.

Critically, the uncertainty mechanism provides graceful degradation rather
than silent failure in all three scenarios.
Under family holdout, $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ rises on unseen families
(Supplementary Section~S-VI), and the triage policy routes these samples to
\textsc{Escalate} with escalation rates of $32$--$38\%$ for the
lowest-recall Conti sub-variants (Table~\ref{tab:ood_variants}).
Under concurrent execution, escalation rate exceeds $60\%$ for all
\texttt{mix} combinations under Bayesian TPE thresholds
(Figure~\ref{fig:mix}).
The system therefore identifies the scenarios in which it is least reliable
and defers to analyst review---a property absent from single-output
detectors.

\subsection{Additional Limitations}

\textbf{Dataset scope.}
All RanSMAP families range from WannaCry (2017) to Conti (2022); post-2022
families (Cl0p, BlackCat/ALPHV, LockBit 3.0) are not represented.
This is an acknowledged scope decision: RanSMAP is the only publicly
available hypervisor-level telemetry dataset with family labels and
multi-split evaluation protocols at time of writing.
Extension to current-generation families is listed as future work.

\textbf{Adversarial robustness.}
An adaptive attacker aware of the triage policy could craft executions that
suppress $\sigma_{\max}$ below $\kappa$ to avoid Escalation while keeping
$\hat{p}$ below $\tau_{\text{high}}$ to avoid Auto-Contain.
Adversarial attacks against MC Dropout uncertainty
estimates~\cite{gal2016dropout} are a known open problem; we treat
adversarial robustness as a direction for future work.

\textbf{Knowledge Agent sample size.}
The RansKnow corpus~\cite{kabuye2025ransknow} contains $307$
threat-intelligence transcripts (${\approx}215$ training / $46$ validation /
$46$ test after a 70/15/15 split), which is small for a CNN classifier.
The Knowledge Agent's primary role is providing ATT\&CK-level complementary
signal; generalisation metrics for this agent should be interpreted in light
of this data limitation.

\subsection{Comparison with Hirano and Kobayashi~\cite{hirano2025ransmap}}

The closest direct comparator operates on the same hypervisor-level telemetry.
Hirano and Kobayashi\ use a 1D-CNN+LSTM on 23D features with a $30$-second window
and report F-score; on the hardware-holdout split they achieve F-score $0.856$
while our Behavioural CNN achieves AUROC $0.939$ (Table~\ref{tab:generalisation}).
These are different metrics---F-score is threshold-dependent while AUROC
is threshold-independent---so this comparison is indicative rather than a
direct numerical claim of superiority.
On the Conti sub-variant partition, our Behavioural CNN achieves mean recall
$0.68$ versus their $0.56$, using seven fewer input dimensions and no LSTM.
More importantly, where both frameworks struggle, ours escalates rather than
misclassifies: $\sigma_{\text{beh}}$ reliably identifies the most uncertain
samples (Table~\ref{tab:ood_variants}), preserving analyst oversight precisely
where the model is least reliable.
An extended comparison is provided in Supplementary Section~S-IV.

\subsection{Adaptive Thresholding}

The Bayesian TPE policy sets thresholds $(\tau^*, \kappa^*)$ once on the
calibration split; extending this to a Bayesian posterior updated online from
rolling analyst feedback would allow thresholds to track distribution shift
without retraining base agents (see Supplementary Section~S-V).
We defer this extension to future work.

\subsection{Runtime and Deployment}

End-to-end inference latency is $6.71\,\text{ms}$ median per batch of $32$
samples on an NVIDIA GB10 GPU ($T{=}10$, p95 $7.24\,\text{ms}$;
Table~\ref{tab:latency}), supporting $\approx 47{,}000$ concurrent streams
per GPU at a $10$-second window under batch inference.
The Behavioural CNN ($8{,}385$ parameters, $32.8\,\text{KB}$) runs without
a GPU; the three agents parallelise before fusion, so end-to-end latency is
bounded by the slowest agent rather than their sum.
Per-sample complexity is $\mathcal{O}(T \cdot \max_i P_i)$; offline training
(CTGAN, TPE, EWC) is incurred once at deployment.
Full derivations are in Supplementary Section~S-VII.

% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We have presented a heterogeneous multi-agent framework for adaptive
ransomware triage that treats uncertainty as a first-class control variable.
The four principal contributions---MC Dropout per-agent uncertainty, Decision
MLP fusion with CTGAN augmentation, Bayesian TPE triage policy optimisation,
and Peer Consultation with EWC continual learning---collectively address the
structural limitations of single-modality binary detectors. The framework
achieves strong static-PE detection (AUROC $0.996$) and runtime behavioural
detection (AUROC $0.961$), reduces unnecessary escalation by $70\%$ over
fixed-threshold baselines, and maintains calibration (ECE $0.018$) sufficient
for trustworthy autonomous containment decisions. When the system encounters
novel families or concurrent execution patterns beyond its training
distribution, epistemic uncertainty $\sigma_{\max}$ rises and the triage
policy routes to \textsc{Escalate}---preserving analyst oversight precisely
where the model is least reliable. Formal distribution-free coverage
guarantees via Mondrian conformal prediction provide a statistical assurance
complementing the operational triage policy.

Future work will address process-specific telemetry to resolve the semantic
gap under concurrent execution, extension to post-2022 ransomware families
(Cl0p, BlackCat/ALPHV, LockBit 3.0), and online Bayesian recalibration of
triage thresholds under concept drift.
